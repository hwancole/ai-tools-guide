AI can take you from a blank screen to a full first draft of a presentation in a few minutes. What it can't do is decide what your audience needs to hear, or check that every number on a slide is right. The best results come from giving the AI your real content and treating what it makes as a draft you edit.

> **Quick answer:** If you pay for Microsoft 365 Personal or Family, open PowerPoint and ask **Copilot** to create the presentation, ideally from a Word document with your content. If you have Google AI Pro, use **Gemini in Google Slides**. With no paid plan, ask **Claude** (free) to build a downloadable .pptx file, or install the **ChatGPT for PowerPoint** add-in, which has limited free usage. The no-subscription fallback that always works: ask any chatbot for a slide outline, then paste it into PowerPoint's Outline View, which turns each line into a slide.

All plan names, prices and limits below are as of October 2026 and come from each company's own pages (linked at the end).

## Before you start: get your content ready

Whatever tool you use, the draft is only as good as what you give it. Spend five minutes on this first:

1. **Your goal in one sentence.** "Get my manager to approve a second hire" is better than "team update."
2. **Who's watching** and what they already know.
3. **How long you'll talk.** As a rough guide, many people plan about one slide per minute or two of speaking.
4. **Your source material:** notes, a report, a Word document, a spreadsheet of numbers, last year's deck.
5. **Your template**, if your school or company has one. Open it first so the AI uses its fonts and colors.

Remove anything you shouldn't paste into an online service, such as customer names or private figures, unless your organization has approved the tool for that data.

## Option 1: Copilot in PowerPoint (Microsoft 365)

Best if you already pay for Microsoft 365 and present in PowerPoint.

**Who has it:** Microsoft says Copilot is available in Microsoft 365 Personal ($99.99/year), Family ($129.99/year) and Premium ($199.99/year) in the US. On Family and Premium, only the subscription owner gets Copilot; shared members don't. These plans use monthly **AI credits**, so heavy use can hit a limit until it resets. Premium gives much more usage. Work and school accounts depend on what your organization has licensed.

**How to do it:**

1. Open PowerPoint and start a new presentation (or open your template).
2. Select the **Copilot** icon to open the Copilot chat pane.
3. Describe the presentation you want: topic, audience, number of slides, tone.
4. Answer Copilot's follow-up questions, such as who the audience is, or skip them.
5. Copilot builds the slides. Then ask for changes in the same pane: "make slide 4 less wordy," "add a summary slide."

**Tip:** Copilot works especially well from a Word document. Microsoft recommends files under 24 MB and using Word's heading styles, which help Copilot split the content into slides.

## Option 2: Gemini in Google Slides

Best if you use Google Slides and have a Google AI plan.

**Who has it:** Google says Gemini in Slides needs an eligible Google Workspace or Google AI plan. Creating a **whole multi-slide presentation** from a prompt is available on Google AI Pro and Ultra and on Workspace Business Standard and Plus, Enterprise Standard and Plus. At launch it worked in English (United States) only, and it was still rolling out to some accounts.

**How to do it:**

1. Open a new presentation in Google Slides.
2. Click **Ask Gemini** (top right) to open the side panel.
3. Type a prompt describing the deck. You can type **@** and a file name to point Gemini at a Doc, Sheet or another file in your Drive.
4. Answer Gemini's questions about tone, style or audience, and approve or edit the outline before it builds the slides.

If you only need one slide at a time, use **Insert > Help me create a slide**. To make an illustration, open **Tools > Create images**. Google notes that images generated there are for use only within Google Slides.

## Option 3: Claude (free plan works)

Best if you don't pay for anything and want a real PowerPoint file.

**Who has it:** Anthropic says file creation is available on every Claude plan, including Free, and Claude can create PowerPoint presentations (.pptx). Pro costs $20/month (or $17/month billed yearly) for more usage.

**How to do it:**

1. In claude.ai, go to **Settings > Capabilities** and make sure **Code execution and file creation** (shown as "Create and edit files" in newer layouts) is turned on.
2. Start a chat, upload your notes or report, and describe the deck. Ask explicitly for "a downloadable PowerPoint (.pptx) file."
3. Download the file from the conversation (or save it to Google Drive) and open it in PowerPoint or Google Slides to polish.

Paid Claude plans (Pro and up) can also use the **Claude for PowerPoint** add-in, which works inside PowerPoint on the web, Windows and Mac, follows your template's layouts and can turn bullet points into editable charts and diagrams.

## Option 4: ChatGPT for PowerPoint add-in

Best if you use ChatGPT and want it working directly inside your deck.

**Who has it:** OpenAI's add-in is available on Free, Go, Plus, Pro and business plans. Free and Go get limited usage.

**How to do it:**

1. In PowerPoint, go to **Home > Add-ins** and search for ChatGPT.
2. Install it, open the sidebar and sign in with your ChatGPT account.
3. Ask it to build a deck from your source material, or to revise specific slides.

OpenAI's own advice: for big edits, ask for a plan first ("outline which slides you would change and why"), and duplicate the file before important changes so you can go back.

## The free method that works with any chatbot

No add-ins, no subscription, works with ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot or anything else:

1. Ask the chatbot for an outline using the outline prompt below. Ask for slide titles on their own lines and bullets indented under them.
2. Open a blank presentation in PowerPoint and go to **View > Outline View**.
3. Paste the outline into the outline pane. Each top-level line becomes a slide title. Put the cursor on a line and press **Tab** to turn it into a bullet under the slide above, or **Shift+Tab** to promote it back to a slide.
4. Apply a theme from the **Design** tab, then ask the chatbot for speaker notes and paste them into the notes area of each slide.

## Copy-paste prompts

**1. Plan before building**

> I need a [number]-slide presentation for [audience] about [topic]. My goal is to [get approval / teach / update]. I'll talk for [minutes] minutes. Before making slides, ask me up to 5 questions about my audience and content. Then propose an outline: slide titles plus one sentence on the point each slide makes.

**2. Turn a document into slides**

> Turn the attached document into a [number]-slide presentation. One idea per slide. Slide titles should state the takeaway ("Costs fell 12% after the switch"), not just the topic ("Costs"). Maximum 4 bullets per slide, maximum 10 words per bullet. Use only facts and numbers from the document, and mark anything you're unsure of with [CHECK].

**3. Outline for pasting into PowerPoint**

> Write an outline for a presentation on [topic] for [audience]. Format: each slide title on its own line with no numbering or symbols, and each bullet on the next lines indented with one tab. 8 to 10 slides. Start with a title slide and end with a clear next step.

**4. Speaker notes**

> Write speaker notes for each slide of this outline. About [60] seconds of speaking per slide, in plain conversational language. Add one transition sentence leading into the next slide.

**5. Make a slide visual**

> This slide is too text-heavy: [paste text]. Suggest two ways to show the same information visually (a chart, a diagram, a timeline, a comparison) and say what data or labels each would need.

**6. Practice the Q&A**

> Act as a skeptical [manager / teacher / client] watching this presentation. List the 7 hardest questions I'm likely to get, and for each one, what a strong short answer would include.

## Check before you present

AI drafts look finished long before they are. Go through every slide:

- **Every number and date is real** and matches your source. Delete anything the AI invented.
- **Each slide makes one point**, and the title says what that point is.
- **Text is readable from the back of the room.** Cut bullets rather than shrinking fonts.
- **Images are appropriate** and you have the right to use them. Check the terms of the tool that made them.
- **Charts are labelled** with units and a source.
- **The story flows**: problem, what you found or propose, what you want the audience to do.
- **It fits your time.** Rehearse once out loud with a timer.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Asking with no content.** "Make a presentation about our sales" gets you generic filler. Give it your numbers.
- **Accepting the first design.** Ask for changes, or apply your own template and let the AI handle text.
- **Reading the slides aloud.** Put the detail in speaker notes and keep the slides short.
- **Too many slides.** AI tends to produce more than you need. Ask it to cut to the essentials.
- **Pasting sensitive data into a personal account.** Use the tools your organization approves for work material.

## FAQ

**Can AI make a PowerPoint for free?**
Yes. As of October 2026, Claude's free plan can create a downloadable .pptx file, and the ChatGPT for PowerPoint add-in has limited free usage. Any free chatbot can also write an outline you paste into PowerPoint's Outline View.

**Is Copilot in PowerPoint free?**
No. It comes with paid Microsoft 365 Personal, Family and Premium plans (or an organization's license), and usage is limited by monthly AI credits. On Family and Premium, only the subscription owner can use it.

**Can Gemini make a whole Google Slides presentation?**
Yes, on Google AI Pro, Ultra and eligible Workspace plans. At launch, whole-deck creation was available in US English only. Single slides can be created with **Help me create a slide**.

**Will the slides use my company template?**
Usually, if you open the template first. Copilot and Claude for PowerPoint both work from your existing layouts, and Gemini creates slides in the current theme. Always check fonts and colors afterward.

**Can AI add charts to my slides?**
Some tools can. Claude for PowerPoint can turn bullets into editable native charts. With other tools, you may need to build the chart in Excel or Sheets from your real data and paste it in.

**Is it okay to use AI for a school presentation?**
Follow your teacher's or school's policy. Where it's allowed, using AI to organize your own research and practice questions is usually fine; you're still responsible for the facts and for presenting it yourself.

**Which AI is best for presentations?**
The one that sits where you work. If you live in PowerPoint and pay for Microsoft 365, use Copilot. In Google Slides with Google AI Pro, use Gemini. With no subscription, Claude or the ChatGPT add-in are the practical free choices.
