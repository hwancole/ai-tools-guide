You don't need a special "PDF summarizer" app. The free versions of ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini can all read a PDF you upload and summarize it in seconds. What makes the difference between a vague summary and a useful one is how you ask, and knowing each tool's limits for long or scanned files.

> **Quick answer:** Open ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini, click the **+** (or paperclip) button next to the message box, upload your PDF, and ask: *"Summarize this PDF for [who it's for]. Give a 3-sentence overview, the 5 key points, and any numbers, dates or deadlines."* Then check anything important against the original page, because AI summaries can miss or misstate details.

## Step by step

1. **Open the chatbot** in your browser or app: ChatGPT (chatgpt.com), Claude (claude.ai) or Gemini (gemini.google.com). A free account is enough for most documents.
2. **Attach the PDF.** Click the **+** or paperclip icon next to the message box and choose the file from your computer or phone. Gemini can also import files from Google Drive.
3. **Tell it who the summary is for and what you need.** "Summarize this" works, but "Summarize this lease for a first-time renter and list anything I'd pay extra for" works far better.
4. **Ask follow-up questions** in the same chat: "What does section 4 say about cancellations?" or "Quote the exact sentence about the deposit." The AI keeps the document in the conversation.
5. **Verify.** Ask it to point to the page or section for each key claim, then open the PDF and check the ones that matter.

## File limits by tool (as of October 2026)

These are the limits each company publishes in its own help pages. They change often, so check the linked help page if a file won't upload.

| | ChatGPT | Claude | Gemini |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Max file size** | 512 MB per file | 500 MB per file | 100 MB per file |
| **Length limit** | 2 million tokens per file | 1,000 pages per PDF | About 1,500 pages of text with a 1M-token context window (Google AI Pro or Ultra) |
| **Free plan** | Uploads allowed, but only 3 files per day | Uploads allowed, with lower usage limits than paid plans | Uploads allowed; paid plans get higher limits |
| **Files at once** | No per-chat number published | Up to 20 per chat | Up to 10 per prompt |
| **Charts and images inside the PDF** | Text only on most plans (images are discarded; only Enterprise reads visuals) | Text and visuals for PDFs up to 100 pages; text only for 101 to 1,000 pages | Not specified per file type |

**What this means in practice:**

- **Everyday PDFs** (a contract, a school reading, a 20-page report): any of the three will work fine on a free plan.
- **Several PDFs a day on a free plan:** ChatGPT's free plan caps uploads at 3 files per day, so Claude or Gemini may be easier if you summarize a lot.
- **PDFs where charts, tables-as-images or diagrams matter:** Claude reads visual elements on PDFs up to 100 pages. On most ChatGPT plans, only the digital text is used.
- **Very long documents** (a book, a 600-page manual): all three can take long files, but summaries of huge documents get shallow. Summarize chapter by chapter instead (see below).

## Copy-paste prompts

**General summary**

> Summarize the attached PDF for [audience, e.g. "a busy manager" or "a high school student"]. Give me: 1) a 3-sentence overview, 2) the 5 most important points as bullets, 3) every number, date, deadline or amount mentioned, with the page number. Don't add anything that isn't in the document.

**Contracts, leases and terms**

> Summarize this [lease/contract/policy] in plain English for someone with no legal background. List: what I must do, what I must pay and when, how to cancel, penalties, and anything unusual compared to a typical agreement of this kind. Quote the exact wording for each item and give the section number.

AI is not a lawyer. Use this to understand a document and prepare questions, not as legal advice.

**Study notes from a textbook chapter or paper**

> Turn this chapter into study notes: the main argument in 2 sentences, the key terms with one-line definitions, the 3 ideas most likely to be on an exam, and 5 practice questions. Wait for my answers before showing the solutions.

**Research paper**

> Summarize this paper: research question, method, sample, main findings with the actual numbers reported, limitations the authors mention, and what it does NOT show. Keep it under 250 words.

**Meeting packs and reports**

> I have 10 minutes before a meeting about this report. Give me the 3 decisions it asks for, the strongest argument for and against each, and 3 smart questions I could ask.

## How to summarize a very long PDF

Even when a tool accepts a 500-page file, asking for one summary of the whole thing tends to give a generic overview. Do it in layers:

1. **Ask for the structure first:** "List the chapters or main sections of this PDF with page ranges."
2. **Summarize section by section:** "Now summarize chapter 3 (pages 41 to 78) in 10 bullet points."
3. **Combine at the end:** "Using the chapter summaries above, write a one-page summary of the whole book."

If a file is rejected as too large, split it with any free PDF tool (or your computer's built-in print-to-PDF with a page range) and upload the parts one at a time in the same chat.

## What about scanned PDFs?

A scanned PDF is really a set of pictures of pages, with no selectable text. You can test this by trying to highlight a word in your PDF viewer: if you can't select the text, it's scanned.

- **Claude** analyzes visual content for PDFs up to 100 pages, so short scans usually work.
- **ChatGPT** extracts only the digital text from PDFs on most plans, so a scanned PDF may come back empty or wrong. Workarounds: run OCR first (many PDF apps, and Google Drive's "Open with Google Docs", can do this), or upload the pages as images, which ChatGPT can read.
- **Gemini** accepts PDFs and images; if a scan gives poor results, try uploading clear photos of the pages instead.

Whatever tool you use, check names, numbers and amounts carefully on scans, because blurry text is where mistakes creep in.

## Privacy: think before you upload

Uploading a file sends it to the company running the chatbot. Before you upload:

- **Don't upload** documents with ID numbers, bank details, medical records or confidential work files unless your employer or school has approved that tool for it.
- **Check your data settings.** ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini each have settings that control whether your chats can be used to improve their models. Look in the tool's settings or privacy page.
- **Remove what you don't need.** If you only need a summary of the terms, delete pages with personal details first, or copy and paste just the relevant section.

## Mistakes that lead to bad summaries

- **No audience or goal.** "Summarize" alone gives a generic abstract. Say who it's for and what decision or task it supports.
- **Trusting numbers without checking.** AI can misread tables, mix up figures from different pages, or state something that isn't in the document. Ask for page numbers and check the important ones.
- **One huge request.** Asking for a summary, a translation, a slide outline and a quiz at once gives four weak answers. Do them one at a time.
- **Starting a new chat for every question.** Keep asking in the same conversation so the AI still has the document in front of it.

## FAQ

**Can I summarize a PDF with AI for free?**
Yes. As of October 2026, the free plans of ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini all accept PDF uploads. ChatGPT's free plan limits uploads to 3 files per day; Claude and Gemini have usage limits that are lower on free plans than on paid ones.

**Which AI is best for summarizing PDFs?**
For ordinary text PDFs, all three give good summaries when you use a specific prompt. Claude has the clearest published support for charts and visuals inside PDFs (up to 100 pages), Gemini fits well if your files are already in Google Drive, and ChatGPT works well for text documents if you stay within its free daily upload cap. Try the same prompt in two tools on an important document and compare.

**Is it safe to upload a PDF to ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini?**
It's like sending the file to an online service: fine for public or low-risk documents, risky for private or confidential ones. Check the tool's privacy settings and your organization's rules before uploading sensitive files.

**Why does the AI say it can't read my PDF?**
Common reasons: the file is too large or too long for that tool, it's password-protected, or it's a scan with no text layer. Try removing the password, splitting the file, or running OCR first.

**Can AI summarize a PDF in another language?**
Yes. Add "Write the summary in English" (or any language) to your prompt. For important documents, ask it to quote the original sentence next to each translated point so you can double-check.

**Can I trust the summary without reading the PDF?**
For low-stakes reading, a good summary is often enough. For contracts, exams, medical, legal or financial documents, use the summary as a map, then read the sections that matter yourself.
