AI is very good at the hardest part of a resume: turning "I did a bit of everything at my last job" into clear, specific bullet points. It is also very good at quietly inventing things you never did. The trick is to give it your real history, let it organize and phrase, and keep yourself in charge of every fact.

> **Quick answer:** Open ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini (a free account is enough). Paste the job posting, then paste your old resume or a rough list of your jobs, dates and what you actually did. Ask the AI to interview you with questions to fill the gaps, then to write achievement-style bullet points using only your answers. Put the result into a simple one-column template in Word or Google Docs, check every line is true, and adjust it for each job you apply to.

## What to gather first

The quality of the resume depends on the raw material you give the AI. Collect:

1. **Your current resume**, if you have one, even if it's old.
2. **A list of every job, internship or volunteer role**: title, organization, city, start and end month.
3. **Rough notes on what you did in each role.** Bullet fragments are fine: "ran the weekend shift," "fixed the booking spreadsheet," "trained 3 new hires."
4. **Any results you can actually back up**: money saved, time saved, number of customers, ratings, awards. Leave numbers out if you don't know them.
5. **Education, certificates and tools** you use (software, languages, machines, licences).
6. **The job posting** you're aiming at. A resume tailored to one posting does much better than a general one.

Before pasting anything, remove what an online service doesn't need: ID numbers, date of birth, full street address. Your city, email and phone number are usually enough on a resume anyway.

## Step by step

1. **Start a new chat** at chatgpt.com, claude.ai or gemini.google.com.
2. **Paste the job posting first**, then your old resume or notes. You can also upload the file with the **+** or paperclip button.
3. **Let the AI interview you.** Use the interview prompt below. Answer its questions in plain words; this is where the specific details come from.
4. **Ask for the draft** with the main resume prompt.
5. **Fact-check every line** using the checklist further down. Delete or fix anything you didn't say.
6. **Format it** in a clean template (see the formatting section) and export as PDF unless the employer asks for Word.
7. **Tailor it for each new job** with the tailoring prompt. It takes a few minutes per application.

## Copy-paste prompts

**1. Interview me first (best starting point)**

> I'm writing a resume for this job posting: [paste posting]. Here is my current resume / my notes: [paste]. Before writing anything, ask me up to 10 short questions, one group at a time, to find specific achievements, tools, and results I've left out. Focus on what this employer seems to care about most.

**2. Write the resume**

> Using only the information in my notes and my answers above, write a one-page resume for the [job title] role. Sections: short summary (2–3 lines), experience, skills, education. For each job, write 3–5 bullet points that start with a strong verb and show what I did and what happened as a result. Use numbers only where I gave them. Use keywords from the job posting where they truthfully match my experience. Don't add any skill, tool, title, number or achievement I didn't mention. Mark anything you're unsure about with [CHECK].

**3. Turn duties into achievements**

> Rewrite these bullet points so they show results instead of duties. If a bullet has no result, ask me what happened rather than inventing one: [paste bullets]

**4. Tailor an existing resume to a new job**

> Here is my resume and a new job posting. List the 5 requirements that matter most in the posting and show where my resume matches each one. Then suggest specific edits: which bullets to move up, reword or cut, and which posting keywords I could truthfully add. Don't rewrite the whole resume.

**5. Career change**

> I'm moving from [old field] to [new field]. Rewrite my summary and bullet points so they highlight transferable skills for this posting: [paste]. Keep job titles and dates exactly as they are.

**6. First resume (student or no experience)**

> I'm a [student / recent graduate] with little work experience. Build a one-page resume from my coursework, projects, volunteering, part-time jobs and skills below. Put the strongest section first. [paste list]

**7. Get a recruiter-style review**

> Act as a recruiter hiring for this posting. Review my resume and give me the 5 most important problems, in order, with a suggested fix for each. Be direct.

## Which AI tool should you use?

All three major chatbots write solid resume drafts on their free plans, so use the one you already have. The practical differences are about files (as of October 2026, from each company's own pages):

| | ChatGPT | Claude | Gemini |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Free plan** | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| **Upload your old resume** | Yes; free accounts can upload up to 3 files per day | Yes; PDF and DOCX supported, up to 20 files per chat | Yes, when signed in; up to 10 files per prompt |
| **Good to know** | Everyday text chat is unlimited on Free, so pasting text avoids the file cap | The free plan lists file creation, so you can ask for a downloadable document | Can pull a resume straight from Google Drive once you connect it |
| **Paid plan** | Higher limits | Pro is $20/month (or $17/month billed yearly) | Google AI Pro and Ultra raise limits |

If you hit a limit, copy the text of your resume and paste it in. For a resume, text works just as well as the file. Microsoft Copilot and other assistants handle this task the same way; the prompts matter more than the tool.

## Fact-check before you send

AI drafts often "upgrade" your experience to sound more impressive. Read every line and check:

- **Job titles, employers and dates** are exactly right. These are the easiest things for an employer to verify.
- **Every number is real.** If the AI wrote "increased sales by 25%" and you never said that, delete it.
- **Every skill is one you'd be comfortable being tested on** in an interview.
- **Verbs match your actual role.** "Led" and "managed" mean you were in charge. If you helped, say "supported" or "worked with."
- **Search for [CHECK] markers** from the prompt above and resolve each one.
- **No copied phrases from the posting that aren't true for you.** Matching keywords helps only when the match is real.

## Formatting that works with ATS

Many employers use an applicant tracking system (ATS) that reads your resume before a person does. Simple layouts are read most reliably:

- **One column**, standard section headings (Experience, Education, Skills).
- **No text inside images, tables or text boxes.** Some systems skip them.
- **A common font** and normal-size text.
- **Your contact details in the main body**, not only in the page header.
- **PDF or DOCX**, whichever the application asks for. When in doubt, PDF keeps your layout intact for human readers; DOCX is safer if the posting specifically requests Word.

Word and Google Docs both include free resume templates (in Google Docs, go to **File > New > From template**). Pick the plainest one, then paste your AI-written text into it.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Asking for a resume with no information.** "Write me a resume for a nurse" gives you a fictional nurse.
- **Sending the same resume everywhere.** Use the tailoring prompt for each posting.
- **Keeping the AI's generic summary.** Lines like "results-driven professional with a proven track record" add nothing. Replace them with what you do and for whom.
- **Going too long.** One page is usual for early-career resumes; two pages is common with longer experience. Ask the AI to cut if needed.
- **Forgetting country conventions.** Photos, date of birth and length expectations differ by country. Tell the AI which country you're applying in.

## FAQ

**Is it okay to use AI to write my resume?**
For most jobs, yes. Using AI to organize and phrase your own experience is similar to using a template or asking a friend for edits. What matters is that everything on it is true. If an employer or program states its own rules about AI use, follow them.

**Can employers tell a resume was written by AI?**
Detection tools aren't reliable, but recruiters notice generic wording and vague bullets. A resume built from your specific details and edited by you doesn't read that way.

**Can I write a resume with AI for free?**
Yes. As of October 2026, the free plans of ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini all handle this. ChatGPT's free plan limits file uploads to 3 per day, which you can avoid by pasting text.

**Which AI is best for resumes?**
ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini all write good drafts when you give them your real details and the job posting. Use the one you have. Running the same prompt in two tools and combining the best bullets is a fair way to get options.

**Will AI make my resume pass ATS?**
AI can help you use the posting's keywords where they honestly fit. Passing ATS also depends on simple formatting and on actually matching the job's requirements, which no tool can fake.

**Should I let AI write my whole resume or just parts?**
Give it everything and let it draft, but treat the result as a first draft. The interview step and your fact-check are what make it accurate.

**Can AI write a resume in another language?**
Yes. Say which language and country it's for, since formats differ. Have a fluent speaker review it before you apply.
