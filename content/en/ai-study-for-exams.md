AI is most useful for exam prep when it makes you do the thinking: quizzing you, explaining what you got wrong and spacing out your review. It is least useful when it just hands you answers, because reading a perfect explanation feels like learning but rarely sticks on test day.

> **Quick answer:** Upload your own notes, slides or syllabus to an AI chatbot and ask it to **quiz you, one question at a time, and explain your mistakes**. ChatGPT (Study mode), Gemini (Guided Learning) and Claude (the Learning skill) all have a built-in tutoring mode for this, and Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM) can turn your materials into flashcards and quizzes. Always check facts against your course materials, because AI can be confidently wrong.

## Which tool to use (as of October 2026)

All four options below have a free way in. Feature names and menus change often, so if you can't find a button, check the linked help page.

| Tool | Study feature | How to turn it on | Good for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ChatGPT** | Study mode: guides you with questions, explains step by step, checks your understanding, quizzes you | Type **@study** in the message box, or click **+** and choose **Study**. Also at chatgpt.com/studymode. Available on all plans | Working through problems and concepts you don't understand yet |
| **Gemini** | Guided Learning, plus quizzes and flashcards in Canvas | On gemini.google.com, click **Add files > More tools > Guided Learning**. For flashcards, ask "Create flashcards about..." | Visual explanations and quick practice sets |
| **Claude** | Learning skill (it used to be a "style") | Go to **Customize > Skills**, click **+**, choose **Browse skills**, search for **Learning** and install it. Skills work on Free and paid plans | Explaining your reasoning and being challenged on it |
| **Gemini Notebook** (formerly NotebookLM) | Flashcards and quizzes generated only from the sources you upload, with Easy/Medium/Hard difficulty | Create a notebook, add sources, then choose **Flashcards** or **Quizzes** in the Studio panel | Studying a fixed set of readings, lecture notes or a textbook chapter |

A few details worth knowing:

- **ChatGPT Study mode** uses the same message limits as normal chats, and it isn't available in Temporary Chats, GPTs or Projects. You can upload notes, worksheets, slides, PDFs and photos of problems if uploads are available on your plan.
- **Gemini Guided Learning** is rolling out gradually in the mobile app, so the website is the most reliable place to find it.
- **Gemini Notebook** has a free tier with "standard" usage limits that refresh every five hours; Google AI Plus, Pro and Ultra plans get higher limits. Flashcards can be shuffled, marked "Got it" or "Missed it", retaken with only the cards you missed, and downloaded as a CSV file.

If you only want one tool, pick the one you already use. The method below matters more than the brand.

## Step by step: turn your notes into an exam plan

1. **Gather your real materials.** Syllabus, lecture slides, your notes, past papers, the textbook chapters on the test. The AI should work from *your* course, not from a general idea of the subject, because teachers cover topics differently.
2. **Ask for a map of what's on the exam.** Upload the syllabus or review sheet and ask the AI to list every topic, then rate how confident you feel about each one (you do the rating, not the AI).
3. **Test yourself before you review.** Ask for a short diagnostic quiz. Answering questions first, even badly, shows you where your gaps are and makes the later explanations stick better.
4. **Fix the weak topics with a tutor mode.** Switch on Study mode, Guided Learning or the Learning skill and work through each weak topic. Let it ask you questions instead of reading long explanations.
5. **Make flashcards for facts and definitions.** Use Gemini Notebook or ask any chatbot for flashcards from your notes. Review the ones you miss more often than the ones you know.
6. **Do a timed practice exam.** Ask for questions in the same format as your real exam (multiple choice, short answer, essay, problem sets), answer them without help, then ask for marking against a rubric.
7. **Repeat on a schedule.** Come back to missed questions after a day, then after a few days. A short daily session beats one long night before.

## Copy-paste prompts

**Exam map from your syllabus**

> I have an exam on [date] in [subject]. Here is the syllabus / review sheet. List every topic that could be tested, grouped by unit, with the key terms for each. Then ask me to rate my confidence in each topic from 1 to 5, and build a study order starting with my weakest topics.

**Diagnostic quiz**

> Using only the attached notes, give me a 10-question quiz that mixes easy and hard questions across all topics. Ask one question at a time and wait for my answer. After each answer, tell me if I'm right and explain briefly. At the end, list the topics I should review.

**Tutor without giving answers**

> I'm studying [topic] for an exam. Don't give me the answer. Ask me one guiding question at a time, let me try, and only give a hint if I'm stuck twice. Check my understanding at the end by asking me to explain the idea in my own words.

**Flashcards**

> Turn these notes into 25 flashcards. Front: a question, not just a term. Back: a short answer under 25 words. Include the definitions, formulas, dates and cause-and-effect links most likely to be tested. Format as a two-column table I can copy into a flashcard app.

**Practice exam with marking**

> Write a practice exam in the same format as this past paper: [paste or attach]. Same number of questions, same mix of topics, similar difficulty. Don't show answers. When I paste my answers, mark them out of the same total, explain each lost mark and tell me what a full-mark answer would include.

**Essay exams**

> My exam has a 45-minute essay question on [topic]. Give me 5 likely questions. For the one I pick, help me plan an answer: ask me for my thesis and main arguments first, then point out weak spots and missing evidence from my notes. Don't write the essay for me.

## A simple 7-day plan

| Day | What to do with AI | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Exam map from syllabus, confidence ratings, diagnostic quiz | 45 min |
| 2 | Tutor mode on your 2 weakest topics; make flashcards for them | 45 min |
| 3 | Flashcards from day 2 (missed cards only); tutor mode on the next 2 topics | 45 min |
| 4 | Mixed quiz on everything so far; review mistakes | 40 min |
| 5 | Full timed practice exam with no AI help; then ask for marking | 60 to 90 min |
| 6 | Re-study every topic you lost marks on; retake missed flashcards | 45 min |
| 7 | Short mixed quiz, a quick read of your own summary, then rest | 30 min |

Adjust the days to your timetable. If you have less than a week, merge days 2 to 4 and keep the practice exam.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Letting the AI do the work.** If it writes your summaries and answers your practice questions, you'll recognise the material but not be able to produce it in the exam. Ask it to question you instead.
- **Studying from what the AI "knows" instead of your course.** Upload your notes and ask it to stick to them. Course content, notation and marking schemes differ between schools and teachers.
- **Not checking facts.** Chatbots can get dates, formulas and definitions wrong while sounding sure. Check anything you'll memorise against your textbook or notes, especially in maths, science, law and medicine.
- **Breaking your school's rules.** Using AI to study is usually fine; using it on graded homework or during an exam may not be. Check your course's AI policy.
- **Cramming in one session.** A tutor mode doesn't change how memory works. Several shorter sessions spread over days help more than one long one.

## FAQ

**Can I use AI to study for free?**
Yes. As of October 2026, ChatGPT's Study mode is available on all plans including Free, Gemini's Guided Learning and flashcards need only a signed-in Google account, Claude's Learning skill works on the Free plan, and Gemini Notebook has a free tier with standard usage limits. Paid plans mainly raise usage limits.

**What happened to NotebookLM?**
Google renamed NotebookLM to Gemini Notebook in July 2026. It is still a standalone tool, and notebooks also sync with the Gemini app. Flashcards, quizzes and the other study tools are in the Studio panel.

**Is using AI to study cheating?**
Using it to explain topics, quiz yourself and plan revision is generally treated like using a tutor or a study guide. Submitting AI-written work as your own, or using AI during a test, usually breaks academic rules. Your school's policy is the final word.

**Which AI is best for studying?**
There's no single winner. Gemini Notebook is strong when you have a fixed set of readings because its flashcards and quizzes come from your sources. ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude each have a tutoring mode that's good for working through concepts. Try the same diagnostic quiz prompt in two tools and keep the one whose questions feel closest to your real exam.

**Can AI mark my practice essays?**
It can give useful feedback if you give it the question, the marking rubric and an example of a good answer. Treat the score as a rough guide; your teacher's marking scheme is what counts.

**Can I study from a photo of my handwritten notes?**
Usually, yes. Clear photos of notes or textbook pages can be uploaded to ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude. Check the AI read your handwriting correctly before you build flashcards from it.
