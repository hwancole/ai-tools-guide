AI is a fast, patient proofreader. It spots typos, missing words, wrong verb forms and clumsy sentences in seconds, and it can explain why something is wrong. Its weak spot is that, left alone, it "improves" your text far beyond what you asked: it swaps your words for fancier ones, smooths out your personality and sometimes changes the meaning. The trick is to tell it exactly how much it may change, and to make it show its edits.

> **Quick answer (as of November 2026):** Paste your text into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini (free plans are fine) with this instruction: *"Proofread this. Fix only spelling, grammar and punctuation. Don't change my wording or tone. Return the corrected text, then a list of every change."* Read the list and reject anything you disagree with. If you write in Word or Google Docs, run their built-in spelling and grammar check first; Grammarly's free plan also flags spelling and grammar as you type.

## Proofreading vs. editing: decide first

Most bad AI proofreading comes from asking for one thing and getting another. Pick the level before you paste anything:

| Level | What changes | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| **Proofread** | Spelling, grammar, punctuation, typos only | Anything already written the way you want: emails, assignments, applications |
| **Light edit** | The above plus awkward or unclear sentences | Drafts that feel clunky but are basically right |
| **Rewrite** | Wording, structure, length, tone | Rough notes you want turned into polished text |

If you only want a proofread, say "proofread" and "don't rephrase". The word "improve" invites a rewrite.

| Tool | Best for | Cost (as of November 2026) |

A good routine is to let the built-in checker catch the obvious typos, then use a chatbot for a careful second pass on anything important.

## Step 2: Copy-paste prompts

**1. Strict proofread (keeps your voice)**

```
Proofread the text below. Fix only spelling, grammar,
punctuation and obvious typos. Do not change my word choice,
sentence structure or tone, and do not add or remove content.
Return:
1) The corrected text
2) A numbered list of every change: original -> corrected,
   with a short reason.
If something might be intentional (a name, slang, a style
choice), ask instead of changing it.

Text:
[paste]
```

**2. Light edit for clarity**

```
Lightly edit this for clarity. Fix grammar and typos, and
rewrite only sentences that are confusing or very long. Keep
my vocabulary and keep it sounding like me, not more formal.
Mark each rewritten sentence in **bold** so I can review it.

Text:
[paste]
```

**3. Explain my mistakes (to learn, not just fix)**

```
Find the grammar and spelling mistakes in my text but don't
rewrite it. For each one, quote the phrase, explain the rule
in one simple sentence, and give the correct version. At the
end, tell me which 2-3 mistakes I make most often.

Text:
[paste]
```

**4. Match a spelling standard or style**

```
Proofread this using [American / British / Canadian / Australian]
English spelling and punctuation. Use the [Oxford comma / no
Oxford comma]. Keep numbers as [digits / words]. Change nothing
else.

Text:
[paste]
```

**5. Final check before sending**

```
This is going to [my manager / a client / a university
admissions office]. Without rewriting it, check for:
typos, wrong names or dates, repeated words, missing words,
inconsistent formatting, and anything that could sound rude
or unclear. List problems only; don't fix them.

Text:
[paste]
```

Prompt 5 is useful because it gives you a list to act on rather than a new version you have to compare line by line.

## Step 3: Use the three-pass method for important texts

For a job application, an essay or a message to a client, one pass isn't enough:

1. **Mechanics.** Run prompt 1 (strict proofread) and accept the changes you agree with.
2. **Clarity.** Run prompt 2 on the corrected version, but only accept rewrites of sentences you also found awkward.
3. **Facts and names.** Read the final text yourself, out loud if you can. Check every name, date, number and link. AI doesn't know that the meeting is on Thursday, not Tuesday, or that your client spells their name "Kathryn".

## A short example

Original sentence: *"Their going to recieve the report tomorow, and they're feedback will help us finalise the budget."*

A strict proofread should return *"They're going to receive the report tomorrow, and their feedback will help us finalise the budget."* with a list of four changes: "Their" to "They're", "recieve" to "receive", "tomorow" to "tomorrow", "they're" to "their". Notice "finalise" stays, because it's correct British spelling. If the AI changed it to "finalize" without being asked for American spelling, or rewrote the sentence as "The report will be delivered tomorrow, enabling us to finalize the budget," it went beyond a proofread. That's exactly what the change list helps you catch.

## Step 4: Know what AI gets wrong

- **Over-correcting.** It may "fix" deliberate fragments, dialect, slang or a brand name. Tell it what to leave alone.
- **Changing meaning.** A "clearer" sentence can say something slightly different. Compare the corrected version with your original, especially in contracts, instructions and anything with numbers.
- **Missing context errors.** It can't tell that you wrote "15 March" when you meant "15 May".
- **Long texts.** On very long documents, a chatbot may skip parts or quietly shorten them. Proofread in sections of a few pages at a time and ask it to confirm it returned the full text.
- **Inconsistent results.** Running the same text twice can give different suggestions. That's normal; you decide what to keep.

## Is it OK to use AI to proofread schoolwork?

It depends on your school's or instructor's policy, so check it. Many policies treat spelling and grammar help differently from AI writing the content for you, but rules vary widely. Prompt 3 (explain my mistakes) is the safest option for learning: it shows the errors without rewriting your work. ## FAQ

**What's the best free AI for proofreading?**
ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini are all good at grammar and spelling on their free plans, and the prompt matters more than the tool. Grammarly's free plan is handy if you want corrections as you type in your browser and email.

**How do I stop AI from changing my writing style?**
Say "proofread" rather than "improve", tell it not to change your wording or tone, and ask for a list of every change. Pasting a sentence like "Keep it sounding like me, not more formal" also helps.

**Can AI proofread a whole document?**
Yes, but long documents are more reliable in sections. In Word or Google Docs, the built-in checker covers the whole file; with a chatbot, paste a few pages at a time and check that nothing was cut.

**Does AI proofreading work in other languages?**
The major chatbots handle many languages, and you can ask for a specific variety such as Latin American or European Spanish. Built-in checkers in Word and Docs support many languages too, though advanced suggestions can vary by language.

**Is Grammarly better than ChatGPT for grammar?**
They do different jobs. Grammarly underlines issues as you type in many apps; a chatbot is better when you want to paste a full text, control how much changes and get explanations. Many people use both.

**Will a proofread make my text look AI-written?**
A strict proofread that only fixes mistakes doesn't change your style, so it shouldn't. Heavy rewrites with generic phrases are what tend to read as AI.
