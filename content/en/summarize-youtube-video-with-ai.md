A 40-minute lecture, a long tutorial or a podcast episode on YouTube can often be boiled down to a handful of key points. AI tools can do that for you, but they don't all "watch" videos the same way, so the method you pick matters.

> **Quick answer:** The simplest free option is **Gemini**: sign in at gemini.google.com or in the Gemini app, make sure **Keep Activity** is on, paste the YouTube link and ask "Summarize this video." If you're on YouTube already, look for the **Ask** button under the video, which is available on select videos in select countries and languages (as of November 2026). For any other chatbot, such as **ChatGPT**, **Claude** or **Copilot**, the most reliable way is to open the video's transcript (**...more > Show transcript**), copy it, and paste it into the chat with a summary prompt.

## Which method fits you

| Your situation | Best method | What you need |
|---|---|---|
| You want the fastest free option | **Gemini** with the video link | A Google account, Keep Activity turned on |
| You're already watching on YouTube | YouTube's **Ask** button | A signed-in account (13+), a supported country, language and video |
| You prefer ChatGPT, Claude or Copilot | **Transcript + chatbot** | A video with captions |
| The video has no captions | Transcribe the audio first | The video file and the right to use it |
| You need exact quotes or timestamps | **Transcript + chatbot** | Ask the chatbot to quote and cite times |

## Method 1: Summarize a YouTube video with Gemini

Google's Gemini app can use public information from YouTube, which means you can give it a link or ask it to find a video and then ask questions about it. According to Google's Gemini Apps Help (as of November 2026):

- It works on the web (gemini.google.com), on Android, and on iPhone and iPad.
- You must be **signed in** and have **Keep Activity** turned on. Google says the feature isn't available when that setting is off.
- It isn't available in Gemini Live chats or in Gemini inside Google Messages.

**Steps**

1. On YouTube, click **Share > Copy** under the video (or copy the address from your browser bar).
2. Open **gemini.google.com** or the Gemini app and sign in.
3. Paste the link and add a request, for example: *"Summarize this video in 5 bullet points, then list any steps it explains."*
4. Ask follow-up questions in the same chat: *"What did the speaker say about pricing?"* or *"At what point do they explain the second method?"*

You can also skip the link and search by description, for example *"Find a video that explains how compound interest works"*, and then say *"Summarize the first video."* That example workflow comes straight from Google's help page.

If Gemini says it can't access the video, check that Keep Activity is on, that the video is public, and try again in a new chat. If it still fails, use Method 3.

## Method 2: Use the Ask button on YouTube

YouTube has its own AI feature called **Ask**. It lets you ask questions about the video you're watching, such as "What are the main points?" or "Summarize this video", without leaving the page.

**Steps**

1. Open a video while signed in.
2. Tap or click the **Ask** button below the video. (On a smart TV or game console, press up or down to reveal the video details and select the button.)
3. Pick one of the suggested prompts or type your own question.

**Limits to know (as of November 2026, per YouTube Help):**

- It's only available in **select languages and regions**, and only on **select videos**. If you don't see the button, the feature isn't available for that video or account yet.
- You need to be signed in and meet the minimum age (13+, or the applicable age in your country). It isn't available on supervised accounts or YouTube Kids.
- It runs on Android, iOS, computer web browsers and smart TVs.
- YouTube says answers may be inaccurate, and conversations linked to your account are deleted after 45 days.

## Method 3: Copy the transcript into any chatbot

This method works with almost any AI assistant, including ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot and Gemini, because you give the chatbot the actual words spoken in the video. That makes it the most dependable option, and the best one when you need accurate quotes.

OpenAI's and Anthropic's help pages for ChatGPT and Claude list documents, spreadsheets and images as supported uploads, but don't promise that the assistants can watch a YouTube link. A pasted transcript avoids the guesswork.

**Step 1: Get the transcript**

1. On a computer, open the video on YouTube.
2. Click **...more** in the video description to expand it.
3. Click **Show transcript**. A panel opens with the text and timestamps.
4. Select the text in the panel and copy it. (Use the panel's menu to hide timestamps first if you want cleaner text.)

YouTube says transcripts are available for **any video that has captions**. Many videos have automatic captions; if there's no **Show transcript** button, the video has none.

**Step 2: Paste it into your chatbot**

Open ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, paste one of the prompts below, then paste the transcript underneath. Very long transcripts (multi-hour streams, for example) may be too big for a free plan in one go. If that happens, save the text as a .txt file and upload it, or split it into parts and ask for a combined summary at the end.

## Copy-paste prompts

**Quick summary**

> Summarize this YouTube video transcript in 5 bullet points, then a one-sentence takeaway. Only use what's in the transcript. Transcript: [paste]

**Study notes from a lecture**

> Turn this lecture transcript into study notes: a heading for each topic, key terms in bold with a short definition, and 8 practice questions with answers at the end. Keep the lecturer's examples. Transcript: [paste]

**Steps from a tutorial**

> This is a transcript of a how-to video. Extract every step in order as a numbered list, including tools, settings and amounts mentioned. Add the timestamp where each step starts. Mark anything unclear as "check the video". Transcript: [paste]

**Is it worth watching?**

> In 3 sentences, tell me what this video covers and who it's useful for. Then list the 3 most useful moments with timestamps so I can jump to them. Transcript: [paste]

**Claims check**

> List the factual claims made in this video, each with its timestamp. Don't judge them; just list them clearly so I can check them myself. Transcript: [paste]

## Tips for better video summaries

- **Say who the summary is for.** "Explain it for a beginner" or "I already know the basics, skip them" changes the result a lot.
- **Ask for timestamps.** They let you jump to the exact moment and confirm the AI got it right.
- **Check numbers, names and quotes.** Automatic captions often mishear names and figures, and AI tools can misread or fill gaps. Watch the relevant part before relying on it.
- **Don't treat a summary as the full picture.** Diagrams, demonstrations and on-screen text aren't in a transcript. For a visual tutorial, use the summary to find the right moment, then watch that part.
- **Respect creators.** Summaries are great for deciding what to watch and for revision notes. Don't republish someone's video content as your own.

## FAQ

**Can ChatGPT summarize a YouTube video from a link?**
OpenAI's help pages don't document YouTube links as a supported input, so results can be hit or miss. The reliable way is to copy the video's transcript from YouTube (**...more > Show transcript**) and paste it into ChatGPT with a summary prompt.

**What is the best free AI to summarize YouTube videos?**
Gemini is the most direct free option because Google documents YouTube support: sign in, keep Keep Activity on, paste the link and ask for a summary (as of November 2026). The transcript method works with any free chatbot.

**Why don't I see the Ask button on YouTube?**
YouTube offers Ask only in select languages and regions, on select videos, for signed-in users who meet the age requirement. It isn't on supervised accounts or YouTube Kids.

**Can AI summarize a YouTube video without captions?**
Not with the transcript method, because there's nothing to copy. If you have the video file and the right to use it, transcribe the audio first (see our guide to [transcribing audio with AI](https://hwancole.github.io/ai-tools-guide/en/transcribe-audio-with-ai/)), then summarize the transcript.

**Can AI summarize a video in another language?**
Yes. Paste the transcript and ask, for example, "Summarize this Spanish transcript in English." Ask it to keep key quotes in the original language too, so you can check the translation.

**Are AI video summaries accurate?**
Usually good enough to grasp the main points, but not perfect. Both Google and YouTube warn that their AI can make mistakes. Ask for timestamps and verify anything important in the video itself.
