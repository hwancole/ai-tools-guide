AI can write a cover letter in seconds, and that's exactly the problem: if you just type "write a cover letter for a marketing job," you get the same polished, empty letter every other applicant gets. Recruiters read a lot of those. The way to use AI well is to feed it *your* facts and *their* job posting, let it draft, and then edit it until it sounds like you.

> **Quick answer:** Open ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini (the free plans are enough). Paste the full job posting, upload or paste your resume, and add 2 or 3 specific things you've done that match the job. Ask for a draft under 300 words that opens with why you want *this* role and backs every claim with something from your resume. Then cut the clichés, check every fact, and read it out loud before you send it.

## What you need before you start

Gather these first. Ten minutes of prep is what makes the letter specific instead of generic.

1. **The full job posting.** Copy the whole thing, including responsibilities and "nice to have" skills.
2. **Your resume**, as a file (PDF or Word) or pasted text.
3. **Two or three real examples** that match the job: a project, a problem you fixed, a result you can describe. Numbers help only if they're true and you can explain them in an interview.
4. **One honest reason** you want this company or role. Something you read on their site, a product you use, the kind of team or customers they have.
5. **The hiring manager's name**, if the posting or company site shows it.

## Step by step

1. **Start a new chat** in ChatGPT (chatgpt.com), Claude (claude.ai) or Gemini (gemini.google.com).
2. **Give it the material.** Paste the job posting, then upload your resume with the **+** or paperclip button, or paste its text.
3. **Ask it to analyze before it writes.** Use the "match" prompt below. This shows you which of your experiences fit the job best, and it lets you correct the AI before it writes anything.
4. **Ask for the first draft** with the main prompt below, including your examples and your reason for applying.
5. **Edit with follow-up requests** in the same chat: "make the opening less formal," "cut it to 220 words," "replace the second paragraph with my example about the inventory system."
6. **Do a final human pass** using the checklist further down. This is the step that makes the letter yours.

## Copy-paste prompts

**1. Match your experience to the job (do this first)**

> Here is a job posting and my resume. Before writing anything, list the 5 requirements that matter most for this role, and for each one, point to the strongest matching evidence in my resume. Then tell me which requirements I don't clearly meet, so I can decide how to address them. Don't invent experience I don't have.

**2. The main cover letter prompt**

> Write a cover letter for the [job title] role at [company], using only facts from my resume and the notes below.
>
> - Why I want this role: [your honest reason]
> - Example 1: [what you did, and the result]
> - Example 2: [what you did, and the result]
> - Tone: [friendly and direct / formal / warm but professional]
>
> Rules: under 300 words, 3 or 4 short paragraphs. Open with a specific reason I'm interested in this role, not "I am writing to apply." Connect each example to a requirement in the posting. No clichés like "passionate," "dynamic," "proven track record" or "I am confident that." End with a simple, polite next step. Don't add any skill, number or achievement that isn't in my notes or resume.

**3. Career change or gap in your resume**

> I'm moving from [old field] to [new field]. Help me write a cover letter that explains the switch in one or two honest sentences and shows which skills transfer, using these examples: [examples]. Don't apologize for the change or make it sound like a weakness.

**4. First job or internship with little experience**

> I'm a [student / recent graduate] applying for [role]. I don't have much work experience. Use my coursework, projects, volunteering and part-time jobs below to show the skills this posting asks for: [list]. Keep it humble but confident, under 250 words.

**5. Make it sound like me**

> Here is something I wrote myself: [paste an email or message you wrote]. Rewrite the cover letter so it matches my natural style: my sentence length, my level of formality, the kind of words I use. Keep all the facts the same.

**6. Short version for an online form or email**

> Turn this cover letter into a 4-sentence message I can paste into an application form or send as an email to the recruiter.

## Which AI tool should you use?

For a cover letter, all three major chatbots do the job well on a free account, so use the one you already have. The differences that matter here are about uploading your resume (as of October 2026, from each company's help pages):

| | ChatGPT | Claude | Gemini |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Free plan** | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| **Upload a resume file on free plan** | Yes, but free accounts are limited to 3 file uploads per day | Yes; PDF and DOCX are supported | Yes; you must be signed in, and paid plans get higher limits |
| **Good to know** | Free plan includes unlimited everyday text chat, so pasting your resume as text avoids the upload cap | Up to 20 files per chat, handy if you also attach a portfolio piece | Can add files from Google Drive if your resume is already there |

If you hit an upload limit, just copy the text of your resume and paste it into the chat. For a cover letter, plain text works just as well as the file.

Microsoft Copilot and other assistants work the same way: the method and prompts above matter much more than which tool you pick.

## The human pass: how to remove the "AI tone"

AI drafts tend to share the same habits. Before sending, go through this checklist:

- **Cut the warm-up sentences.** Delete openings like "I am excited to apply for..." and start with your actual reason.
- **Replace vague praise with specifics.** "Your innovative company" says nothing. "Your switch to refillable packaging last year" shows you looked.
- **Delete buzzwords.** Watch for "passionate," "synergy," "leverage," "dynamic," "results-driven," "fast-paced environment," "I am confident that."
- **Check every claim.** If the letter says you "led a team" or "increased sales," make sure that's true and that you can talk about it in an interview. AI sometimes upgrades your experience without being asked.
- **Vary the rhythm.** AI drafts often use three-item lists and sentences of the same length. Break a few of them up.
- **Read it out loud.** If a sentence sounds like something you'd never say, rewrite it in your own words.
- **Confirm names and details.** Company name, job title, hiring manager's name and spelling. A wrong company name copied from an earlier letter is an easy way to get rejected.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Sending the first draft.** It's a starting point, not a finished letter.
- **Reusing one letter for every job.** Run the match prompt again for each posting; it takes two minutes.
- **Letting AI invent achievements.** Anything false can come up in the interview, and some employers check references.
- **Going too long.** Most cover letters work best at about one page or less. If the AI writes 500 words, ask it to cut.
- **Pasting private details you don't need to share.** Your resume is fine, but leave out ID numbers, full home address or anything you wouldn't want stored by an online service. Check the tool's privacy settings if you're unsure.

## FAQ

**Is it okay to use AI to write a cover letter?**
For most jobs, using AI as a writing assistant is fine, the same way people have long used templates or asked a friend to edit. What matters is that the content is true and that the final letter reflects you. Some employers or programs (for example certain academic or government applications) have their own rules about AI, so follow them if the posting mentions it.

**Can employers tell if a cover letter was written by AI?**
Detection tools are unreliable, but generic AI letters are easy for people to spot because they sound alike: buzzwords, vague praise and no specific examples. A letter with your real examples and your own phrasing doesn't read that way.

**Which AI is best for cover letters: ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini?**
All three write good drafts when you give them a detailed prompt with the job posting and your resume. Pick the one you already use. If you want a second opinion, paste the same prompt into another tool and combine the best parts.

**Can I write a cover letter with AI for free?**
Yes. As of October 2026, the free plans of ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini all handle this task. ChatGPT's free plan limits file uploads to 3 per day, which you can avoid by pasting your resume as text.

**Should I upload my resume or paste it?**
Either works. Uploading is quicker; pasting the text avoids file upload limits and lets you remove personal details first.

**How long should a cover letter be?**
Usually 250 to 400 words, or less than a page. For online forms with a small text box, use the short-version prompt above.

**Can AI write a cover letter in another language?**
Yes. Ask for the letter in the language of the job posting and say which country it's for, since formats and tone differ. If you aren't fluent, have a native speaker check it before sending.
