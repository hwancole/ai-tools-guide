AI can turn a few facts about a product into a clean, readable description in seconds, and it can rewrite that description for a different platform, tone or length just as fast. What it can't do is know your product. Every claim about size, materials, care or what's in the box has to come from you, or the AI will fill the gaps with plausible guesses.

> **Quick answer (as of November 2026):** Write down the hard facts first: what the product is, who it's for, dimensions, materials, what's included, care instructions and one or two things that make it different. Paste those into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini (the free plans are enough) with a prompt that names the platform, the length and the tone, and tell it not to add any claim you didn't give it. If you sell on Shopify or Amazon, both have a free built-in generator you can use instead. Then read every line against the real product before you publish.

## What AI is good and bad at here

| Good at | Not good at |
|---|---|
| Turning a messy list of facts into tidy copy | Knowing the facts you didn't give it |
| Writing short, medium and long versions at once | Avoiding vague filler ("elevate your style") unless told to |
| Matching a tone: playful, technical, luxury, plain | Knowing your marketplace's rules and character limits for sure |
| Rewriting one description for Etsy, Amazon and your own site | Real SEO data: it can suggest keywords but can't see search volumes |
| Drafting many similar listings from a spreadsheet | Judging whether a health, safety or legal claim is allowed |

The biggest risk is invented details. Shopify's own help page warns that generated text "can include things such as product benefits even when you didn't list any explicitly", and that the merchant is responsible for the accuracy of what they publish. The same applies to any chatbot.

## Step 1: Collect the facts (this is the real work)

A good description comes from a good fact sheet. Before you open any AI tool, write down:

- **What it is**, in plain words (for example "ceramic pour-over coffee dripper").
- **Who it's for** and the main use ("people who brew one or two cups at home").
- **Specs**: size, weight, capacity, materials, colors, compatible models.
- **What's in the box.**
- **Care and safety**: dishwasher safe? hand wash only? age warnings?
- **What makes it different**: handmade, a specific material, a design detail, a guarantee you actually offer.
- **Shipping or personalization details** if they belong in the listing.

If you already have a supplier spec sheet or an old description, paste that in as the fact source. A clear product photo helps too: ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini can all read images, so you can upload one and ask the AI to describe what it sees, but treat that as a draft to check, not as facts.

## Step 2: Pick your tool

| Tool | Best for | Cost (as of November 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| **ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini** | Full control over tone and format, any platform, batches from a spreadsheet | Free plans work; paid plans raise usage limits |
| **Shopify Magic** (in the product editor) | Shopify stores that want a draft right inside the product page | Included with Shopify plans, per Shopify's App Store page |
| **Amazon's listing AI** (Seller Central) | New Amazon listings from a few words, photos or a web page | Part of Seller Central; Amazon's Professional plan is $39.99/month plus selling fees |
| **Etsy's own tools** | Etsy sellers | Check the listing form; Etsy's rules below apply whatever tool you use |

### Shopify

According to Shopify's help center: go to **Products**, open a product, click the **Generate text** icon in the description toolbar, type a prompt with the product's features, keywords and tone, then edit and **Save**. Shopify recommends giving at least a product title and two features. Generation isn't available in the iPhone or Android app, so use a computer.

### Amazon

Amazon's seller blog (November 2025) describes this flow: in Seller Central, go to **Catalog > Add Products**, choose to create a new listing, and use **Generate content**. You can start from a few keywords, up to three product images, or the URL of the product page on your own website (it must be public, not behind a password). Amazon notes that if you submit AI-generated content you agree you've reviewed it, and the URL option only works for new products.

## Step 3: Use a prompt that sets rules

The difference between bland AI copy and useful copy is mostly in the instructions. The prompts below are written to stop the AI from inventing things.

## Copy-paste prompts

**1. Standard product description**

```
Write a product description for my online store.

Product facts (use ONLY these, do not add features, benefits,
certifications or numbers I didn't give you):
[paste your fact sheet]

Audience: [who buys it]
Tone: [friendly / premium / technical / playful]
Format:
- A one-line opening that says what it is and who it's for
- A short paragraph (2-3 sentences) on why it's useful
- 4-6 bullet points with the key specs
- A final line on care or what's in the box
Length: about 120-150 words. Avoid clichés like "elevate",
"game-changer" and "must-have".
If a detail a shopper would expect is missing, list it as a
question at the end instead of guessing.
```

**2. Three versions to choose from**

```
Using the same facts, write 3 versions:
A) short (under 50 words) for a product card or social post,
B) medium (about 120 words) for the product page,
C) long (about 250 words) with a "Why you'll like it" section
and an FAQ of 3 questions a buyer might ask.
```

**3. Rewrite for a specific marketplace**

```
Rewrite this description for [Amazon / Etsy / eBay / my own website].
Give me: a title, 5 bullet points, and a description.
Keep every fact exactly as written. Put the most important
search words near the start of the title, but keep it readable.
Tell me which rules or limits of this marketplace I should
double-check, rather than stating limits you're unsure about.
[paste description]
```

**4. From a photo**

```
Here is a photo of my product. Describe what you can see
(shape, color, visible materials, details) as a bullet list.
Mark anything you are unsure about with "(check)".
Don't guess sizes, materials or brand.
```

Then feed the checked list into prompt 1.

**5. Many products from a spreadsheet**

```
I'm uploading a spreadsheet with one product per row and these
columns: name, category, materials, size, color, price, notes.
For each row, write a 60-80 word description and 4 bullet points
in a consistent style, using only the data in that row.
Return a table with columns: name, description, bullets.
Flag any row where data is missing.
```

Upload a CSV or Excel file; all three major chatbots accept spreadsheets. For more than a few dozen products, work in batches and spot-check each batch.

**6. Improve an existing description**

```
Here is my current product description. Make it clearer and
easier to skim without changing any facts. Cut filler, use
short sentences, and turn specs into bullet points.
Show me what you changed and why.
[paste description]
```

## A quick before-and-after

Facts given: *Handmade ceramic mug, 350 ml, matte white glaze with speckles, dishwasher safe, microwave safe, each one slightly different, ships in recycled packaging.*

A careful AI draft might read:

> **Speckled Ceramic Mug, 350 ml**
> A handmade mug with a soft matte white glaze and natural speckles, sized for a generous coffee or tea.
> - Holds 350 ml
> - Dishwasher and microwave safe
> - Handmade, so each mug's speckles and shape vary slightly
> - Ships in recycled packaging

Notice what isn't there: no "keeps drinks hot for hours", no "lead-free" (true or not, it wasn't in the facts), no "perfect gift for everyone". If you want those claims, add them to the fact sheet only if they're true.

## Step 4: Check before you publish

Read the draft against the product and your fact sheet:

- **Every number** (size, weight, capacity, count) matches.
- **No new claims** about health, safety, eco credentials, awards or certifications you can't back up.
- **No other brands' trademarks** used as if your product were theirs.
- **The title and bullets** fit the marketplace's current limits (check the platform's help pages, since these change).
- **It sounds like your shop.** Change a few phrases so your listings don't read like everyone else's AI copy.

## Marketplace rules on AI (as of November 2026)

- **Etsy** updated its AI rules on October 22, 2026. Every listing must include at least one unedited photo of the actual item against a real background (with some exceptions such as digital and print-on-demand items), AI-generated patterns, recipes and instructions are no longer allowed, bot-generated listings without human involvement are banned, and sellers must disclose when an item is created using AI. Etsy's article doesn't ban using AI to help write listing text, but it suggests noting in the description if you used AI to edit listing photos.
- **Amazon** offers its own AI listing tools and asks you to review what you submit.
- **Shopify** says you're responsible for the accuracy of anything you publish, even when it's generated.

The common thread: AI help with wording is fine, but the listing has to be accurate and a human has to stand behind it.

## FAQ

**Which AI is best for product descriptions?**
For one-off descriptions, ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini all produce good drafts on their free plans when you give them clear facts and rules. If you sell on Shopify or Amazon, the built-in generators save copying and pasting. The quality depends far more on your fact sheet and prompt than on the tool.

**Will Google penalize AI-written product descriptions?**
Google's Search Central guidance says generative AI can be useful for creating content, but that using it to churn out many pages without adding value for users can break its spam policy on scaled content abuse. A thin, generic description that matches hundreds of other stores won't help you whoever wrote it. Accurate, specific details about your product are what make a description useful.

**Do I have to disclose that AI wrote my descriptions?**
None of the marketplace rules covered above require disclosing AI-assisted wording. Etsy does require disclosure when the item itself is created with AI. Rules change, so check your platform's current policies.

**Can AI write descriptions in other languages?**
Yes. Ask for the description in the target language and give it the same facts. Have a native speaker check anything customer-facing, especially sizes, care instructions and legal wording.

**How long should a product description be?**
Long enough to answer a buyer's questions: what it is, who it's for, key specs, what's included and how to care for it. For most products that's roughly 100 to 250 words plus bullet points. Ask the AI for several lengths and pick the one that fits your page.

**Can I use a competitor's description as a template?**
Don't paste a competitor's text and ask for a rewrite; it can still end up too close to the original. Describe the structure you like ("headline, two sentences, five bullets") and use your own facts instead.
