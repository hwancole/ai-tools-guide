Coming up with something to post several times a week is the hard part of social media for most people, whether you run a bakery, freelance, or just want a more active LinkedIn profile. AI chatbots are good at turning a rough idea into a caption, rewriting one post for several platforms, and brainstorming a month of ideas in minutes. What they can't do is know your business, your voice or what actually happened this week, so you supply those.

> **Quick answer (as of November 2026):** Open ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini (the free plans are enough), tell it who you are, who you're writing for, which platform, and the one thing the post should say. Ask for three short versions with a clear call to action and no invented facts, pick one, and edit it so it sounds like you. Paste in two or three of your past posts so it can copy your style. If you post realistic AI-generated video or audio, Meta and TikTok require you to label it.

## What AI does well (and badly) for social posts

| Good at | Not good at |
|---|---|
| Turning bullet points into a readable caption | Knowing what's new in your business this week |
| Writing several versions so you can choose | Sounding like you without examples of your writing |
| Adapting one idea for Instagram, LinkedIn, X and Facebook | Knowing current trends, prices or events unless you tell it |
| Brainstorming a month of post ideas | Judging what your particular audience responds to |
| Shortening, adding a hook, fixing tone | Avoiding clichés, unless you ban them |

The most common problem is generic, over-excited copy: "Exciting news! 🚀", "game-changer", "Don't miss out!" and a wall of hashtags. You fix that with examples and rules in your prompt, not by hoping the tool guesses.

## Step 1: Write a short brief you can reuse

Save this somewhere and paste it at the start of every chat. It takes five minutes once and makes every draft better.

```
About me: [who you are / your business, city, what you sell or do]
Audience: [who follows you and what they care about]
Voice: [e.g. warm and plain, a bit funny, no corporate words]
Avoid: exclamation marks in every sentence, "game-changer",
"exciting news", "elevate", "unlock", more than 1 emoji per post.
Examples of my past posts:
1. [paste a post you liked]
2. [paste another]
```

In ChatGPT you can store this in **custom instructions** or a project, in Claude in a **project**, and in Gemini as a **Gem**, so you don't have to paste it each time. Menu names change often, so look for "personalization", "projects" or "Gems" in settings.

## Step 2: Choose a tool

| Tool | Best for | Cost (as of November 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| **ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini** | Writing and rewriting captions, ideas, calendars | Free plans work for everyday posting; paid plans add higher limits |
| **AI built into design tools** (for example Canva) | Writing a caption next to the graphic you're making | Varies by plan; check the tool |
| **AI in scheduling tools** (Buffer, Hootsuite and others) | Drafting directly where you schedule posts | Varies; usually part of their plans |

Any general chatbot is enough to start. If you need a side-by-side of the big three, see [ChatGPT Plus vs Claude Pro vs Gemini](https://hwancole.github.io/ai-tools-guide/en/chatgpt-plus-vs-claude-pro-vs-gemini/).

## Step 3: Copy-paste prompts

**1. One post from a rough idea**

```
[paste your brief]

Write an Instagram caption about: [what happened / what you want
to say, in plain words]. Facts to include: [price, date, address,
link, etc.].
Rules: start with a hook in the first line, under 100 words,
end with one clear call to action, 3-5 relevant hashtags at the
end, don't invent anything not in my facts.
Give me 3 versions: one plain, one warm, one playful.
```

**2. LinkedIn post from an experience you actually had**

```
[paste your brief]

Turn these notes into a LinkedIn post: [your real notes: what
you worked on, what went wrong, what you learned].
Rules: short first line that makes people click "see more",
short paragraphs, no hashtag spam (max 3), no humblebragging,
no made-up numbers or quotes. End with a question to readers.
```

**3. One idea, every platform**

```
Here is a post I wrote for Instagram: [paste].
Rewrite it for:
- LinkedIn (professional, a little longer, no emojis)
- X (under 280 characters)
- Facebook (friendly, can include the link)
- Threads (conversational, under 500 characters)
Keep every fact identical. Don't add new claims.
```

**4. A month of post ideas**

```
[paste your brief]

Suggest 12 post ideas for the next 4 weeks (3 per week) for
[platform]. Mix: behind the scenes, tips my audience can use,
customer questions answered, and one promotional post per week
at most. For each idea give a one-line hook and what photo or
video I'd need. Put it in a table.
```

**5. Make my draft better without changing my voice**

```
Here's my draft: [paste]. Tighten it, give it a stronger first
line, and fix grammar. Keep my words and personality as much as
possible. Show me what you changed.
```

**6. Hashtags and alt text**

```
For this post: [paste caption + describe the photo]
1) Suggest 5 specific hashtags (mix of niche and local, no
   giant generic ones like #love).
2) Write alt text for the photo in under 125 characters.
```

## Step 4: Fit each platform

Length limits are a common reason AI drafts need trimming. Typical limits as of November 2026:

| Platform | Text limit | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **X** | 280 characters (free accounts) | X Premium allows longer posts, up to 25,000 characters per X's help pages |
| **Threads** | 500 characters | Threads also lets you attach a longer text (up to 10,000 characters) |
| **Instagram** | About 2,200 characters per caption | Only the first line or two show before "more" |
| **LinkedIn** | About 3,000 characters per post | The first couple of lines show before "see more" |

Whatever the limit, the first line does most of the work, because that's all people see in the feed. Ask the AI for "five alternative first lines" and pick the clearest, not the loudest.

## A quick before-and-after

Rough idea: *"new sourdough on Saturdays, 6 euros, sells out by 11"*.

A vague prompt ("write a post about our bread") tends to produce something like "Exciting news! 🍞✨ Our amazing new bread is here! Don't miss out! #bread #bakery #yummy #love". With the brief and prompt 1, you're more likely to get:

> Saturdays now come with sourdough. Long-fermented, crackly crust, €6 a loaf. We bake a limited batch and it's usually gone by 11, so come early or message us to set one aside. #sourdough #[yourcity]bakery #saturdaymorning

Every fact in it came from the owner's note. That's the goal.

## Step 5: Check before you post

- **Every fact is right**: prices, dates, opening hours, links, names.
- **It sounds like you.** Change any phrase you'd never say out loud.
- **No made-up stats or testimonials.** If the AI wrote "loved by thousands", delete it unless it's true and you can back it up.
- **Hashtags are relevant**, not 30 random ones.
- **Images and video**: if they're AI-generated, check the platform's labeling rules below. For making images, see [how to make AI images for free](https://hwancole.github.io/ai-tools-guide/en/make-ai-images-free/).

## Do you have to say you used AI?

For text captions, the platforms below don't require a label. For media, they do in some cases (as of November 2026):

- **Instagram and Facebook (Meta):** Meta's help center says you must label content with **photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio** that was digitally generated or altered, including with AI, before you share it, and that there may be penalties if you don't. You don't have to label AI-created or altered images, though Meta may add a "Made with AI" label if it detects AI. When creating a post or reel, turn on **Label as made with AI**.
- **TikTok:** TikTok requires creators to label AI-generated content that contains **realistic images, audio or video**, and encourages labeling anything completely generated or significantly edited by AI. Turn on **AI-generated content** in the post settings before posting. TikTok may add the label automatically for its own AI effects or for uploads carrying Content Credentials (C2PA), and you can't remove an automatic label.

Separately, if you post about a product you were paid for or given, advertising rules on disclosing sponsorship still apply, whoever wrote the caption.

## FAQ

**Which AI is best for writing social media posts?**
For short captions, ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini are all capable, and the free plans are enough for a few posts a day. The bigger difference is your prompt: a brief with your voice, audience and past examples matters more than the tool.

**Will people be able to tell my post was written by AI?**
They often can when it's full of generic phrases, emojis and hype. Give the AI examples of your writing, ban the clichés you dislike, and edit the final version. A post with a specific detail only you would know rarely reads as AI.

**Is it allowed to use AI-written captions on Instagram, TikTok or LinkedIn?**
Yes. None of these platforms bans AI-assisted text. Labeling rules on Meta's apps and TikTok are about realistic AI-generated video, audio and (on TikTok) images, not captions. You're still responsible for what you post being accurate and following each platform's rules.

**Can AI post to my accounts automatically?**
Some scheduling tools can draft and schedule posts, and chatbots can connect to other apps in some plans. Posting without reading each one is risky: an AI can get a date or price wrong. Reviewing a draft takes seconds.

**How many hashtags should I use?**
There's no single right number. A few specific, relevant hashtags usually serve better than a long list of generic ones. Ask the AI for niche and local tags, then keep the ones that actually describe your post.

**Can I use AI to reply to comments too?**
You can draft replies the same way, but keep them short and personal and check them before posting. The same approach works for reviews: see [how to reply to Google reviews with AI](https://hwancole.github.io/ai-tools-guide/en/reply-to-google-reviews-with-ai/).
