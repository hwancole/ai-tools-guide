You can make good AI images today without paying anything. The big chatbots you may already use, ChatGPT, Gemini and Microsoft Copilot, all create images on their free plans. Adobe Firefly also has a free daily allowance. The catch is that none of them gives unlimited free images, and most don't say exactly how many you get.

> **Quick answer:** Open **ChatGPT**, **Gemini** (gemini.google.com) or **Microsoft Copilot**, sign in with a free account, and type what you want, for example "Create an image of a cozy reading corner by a rainy window, warm light, watercolor style". All three include free image creation as of November 2026, with a limit that resets over time. If you want to edit or restyle a photo you already have, upload it and describe the change. For pictures you'll use in designs or commercial work, **Adobe Firefly** offers free daily generations and is built for design work.

## Which free tool to use

| What you want | Good free choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A quick image from a description | **ChatGPT** or **Gemini** | Type a sentence, refine it by chatting |
| Edit a photo you already have (change the background, add an object, restyle it) | **Gemini** or **ChatGPT** | Upload the photo and describe the change |
| You use Windows or Microsoft 365 | **Microsoft Copilot** | Built into Windows and the Copilot app; free baseline image creation |
| Graphics for a flyer, post or presentation | **Adobe Firefly** | Free daily generations, design-focused tools |
| Lots of images every day | A paid plan | Free limits are designed for occasional use |

## How to make an image in ChatGPT

1. Go to **chatgpt.com** or open the ChatGPT app and sign in with a free account.
2. Type a request that starts with "Create an image of..." and describe the subject, style and setting.
3. Wait for the image to appear. It can take a little longer than a text answer.
4. To change it, just reply: "Make the sky darker", "Remove the person on the left", or "Make it square for Instagram".
5. Click or tap the image to download it.

When you hit the free limit, ChatGPT shows a notice. You can wait for it to reset or upgrade to a paid plan.

## How to make an image in Gemini

1. Go to **gemini.google.com** or open the Gemini app and sign in with a Google account.
2. Type "Create an image of..." with your description. Gemini uses its Nano Banana image model for this.
3. To edit a photo, click the **+** button, upload the image, and say what to change, for example "Replace the background with a beach at sunset, keep the person exactly the same."
4. Keep refining in the same chat until you're happy, then download the image.

Free Gemini images carry a small visible watermark and an invisible SynthID watermark that marks them as AI-generated.

## How to make an image in Microsoft Copilot

1. Open the **Copilot** app on Windows, iPhone or Android, or go to **copilot.microsoft.com**, and sign in with a Microsoft account.
2. Ask: "Create an image of..." and describe what you want.
3. Ask for changes in the same conversation, then download the version you like.

Microsoft describes free image creation as a baseline experience. Microsoft 365 Personal and Family add "higher usage than free", and Microsoft 365 Premium adds "extensive usage", for AI image creation in Copilot.

## How to make an image in Adobe Firefly

1. Go to **firefly.adobe.com** and sign in with a free Adobe account.
2. Choose image generation and type your prompt.
3. Use the settings to pick an aspect ratio (square, landscape, portrait) and a style.
4. Download the result. Your free generations refresh every day.

Adobe says the free plan gives daily generations across image, video and audio, including access to partner models. If you need more, paid Firefly plans include a monthly pool of generative credits.

## How to write a good image prompt

A good image prompt covers five things. You don't need all of them every time, but the more you include, the closer the first result will be.

| Part | Example |
|---|---|
| **Subject** | a golden retriever puppy |
| **Action or setting** | sitting in a pile of autumn leaves in a park |
| **Style** | soft watercolor illustration |
| **Light and mood** | warm afternoon light, calm and cozy |
| **Format** | landscape, 16:9, room for a title at the top |

Put together: "Create an image of a golden retriever puppy sitting in a pile of autumn leaves in a park, soft watercolor illustration, warm afternoon light, calm and cozy, landscape 16:9 with empty space at the top for a title."

## Copy-paste prompts

**Social media post graphic**

> Create a square image for an Instagram post announcing a weekend bake sale. Show a table of cupcakes and cookies in bright pastel colors, flat illustration style, with plenty of empty space at the top for text. Don't add any words to the image.

**Presentation background**

> Create a landscape 16:9 background image for a business presentation about teamwork: abstract shapes in navy and teal, clean and minimal, no people, no text, lighter on the left side so text is readable there.

**Edit your own photo**

> Here is a photo of my living room. Show what it would look like with sage green walls and a large plant next to the sofa. Keep the furniture, layout and lighting exactly the same.

**Product photo for a small shop**

> Using the attached photo of my handmade candle, create a clean product shot on a light wooden table with soft natural window light and a blurred plant in the background. Keep the candle's label and shape unchanged.

**Coloring page for kids**

> Create a black and white coloring page of a friendly dinosaur having a picnic, thick clean outlines, no shading, simple shapes suitable for a 5-year-old.

**Fix a result**

> Keep everything the same, but [make the background plain white / remove the text / change the season to winter / make the person face the camera].

## Tips for better results

- **Change one thing at a time.** When you ask for several changes at once, tools often change parts you wanted to keep.
- **Say what to keep.** "Keep the face and pose exactly the same" helps a lot when editing photos.
- **Ask for no text, then add it yourself.** Words inside AI images can still come out misspelled or distorted, and adding headlines in Canva, PowerPoint or Google Slides gives you full control.
- **Name the format early.** Square for Instagram, 9:16 for stories, 16:9 for slides and YouTube thumbnails.
- **Try a second tool.** If one tool keeps getting it wrong, the same prompt often works better in another.

## What you can't (or shouldn't) do

- **Real people.** Most tools restrict images of real, identifiable people, especially public figures, and creating fake images of real people can be harmful or illegal.
- **Copyrighted characters and logos.** Don't rely on AI images of brands or famous characters for anything public or commercial.
- **Passing AI images off as real photos.** If an image could be mistaken for a real event or a real product photo, say that it's AI-generated. Some tools add watermarks for exactly this reason.
- **Commercial use.** Read the terms of the tool you used before putting an image on a product, ad or book cover. Adobe positions Firefly for commercial design work; for other tools, check their terms.

## FAQ

**What is the best free AI image generator?**
For most people, the one inside the chatbot they already use. ChatGPT, Gemini and Microsoft Copilot all create images for free as of November 2026. Gemini and ChatGPT are especially handy for editing your own photos, and Adobe Firefly suits design work.

**How many free AI images can I make per day?**
None of the major vendors publishes a fixed daily number for free users. ChatGPT tells you when you've reached the free image limit, Gemini's usage limit refreshes every 5 hours up to a weekly cap, and Adobe Firefly's free generations refresh daily. Paid plans raise the limits.

**Can I use free AI images commercially?**
It depends on the tool's terms, and you're responsible for not infringing anyone's rights (for example, by copying a brand or a real person). Check the terms of the specific tool before selling anything that uses the image.

**Why does Gemini add a watermark to my image?**
Google adds a visible watermark and an invisible SynthID watermark to images generated in Gemini so they can be identified as AI-generated.

**Do I need to download an app?**
No. ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot and Firefly all work in a web browser. The phone apps are convenient for editing photos straight from your camera roll.

**Why doesn't the image match what I asked for?**
Usually the prompt was too short or asked for too many things at once. Add the style, setting and format, then fix details one at a time with follow-up messages.
