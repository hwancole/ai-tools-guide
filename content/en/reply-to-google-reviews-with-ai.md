Replying to every review takes time most small businesses don't have, and an angry one-star review is hard to answer calmly. AI chatbots are good at exactly this kind of short, polite writing. They can turn "thanks, glad you liked it" into a reply that sounds human, and they can help you answer a harsh review without sounding defensive. You still need to supply the facts and post the reply yourself.

> **Quick answer (as of November 2026):** Copy the review into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini (the free plans are enough), tell it your business name, what actually happened and the tone you want, and ask for a short reply under about 80 words that doesn't invent anything. Edit it so it sounds like you, then post it from your Google Business Profile (**Read reviews > Reply**). If you manage a single verified profile, Google's Gemini web app can also draft replies to your latest reviews once you connect your Business Profile to it. Never use AI to write reviews of your own business.

## What AI is good and bad at here

| Good at | Not good at |
|---|---|
| Writing a warm, varied thank-you so every reply isn't identical | Knowing what actually happened with that customer |
| Taking the heat out of your first draft to an angry review | Deciding whether to offer a refund or an apology |
| Keeping replies short and professional | Knowing your policies, opening hours or prices unless you tell it |
| Replying in the reviewer's language | Judging legal risk in a dispute |
| Drafting replies for a batch of reviews at once | Sounding like *you* without some editing |

The main risk is the same as with any AI writing: if you don't give it the facts, it fills the gap with plausible inventions, such as a manager's name, a promise to "review our procedures" or a discount you never offered.

## Google's own rules for replies

Google's help center says replies are checked against its content policies before they appear, usually within about 10 minutes but sometimes up to 30 days, and you'll be asked to edit a reply that isn't approved. The reviewer gets a notification when you reply, and you can edit or delete a reply later. You need a verified Business Profile to reply at all.

Google's own tips for writing replies (as of November 2026) are worth putting straight into your prompt:

- Be professional and polite.
- Keep it short and simple.
- Make each reply count: don't send everyone the same "thank you".
- Be conversational, not promotional: don't use replies to push deals.
- For negative reviews: protect the customer's privacy, avoid personal attacks, be honest, apologize when it's appropriate, and reply in a timely way.

Google also says offering incentives for reviews, or for changing or removing a negative review, is strictly prohibited. So no "we'll give you 20% off if you update your review", whoever writes it.

## Step 1: Gather the facts

Before you open a chatbot, jot down a few lines:

- **Your business name** and what you do.
- **What happened**, from your side, if you know (for example "order was 40 minutes late because a driver called in sick").
- **What you can offer**, if anything: a call, a replacement, a refund. Only what you're actually willing to do.
- **How to reach you** offline, such as a general email or phone number (not a staff member's personal details).
- **Your tone**: friendly and casual, warm and formal, short and plain.

## Step 2: Choose your tool

| Tool | How it works | Cost (as of November 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| **ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini** | Paste the review and your facts, copy the draft back into Google | Free plans work for a handful of replies a day |
| **Gemini web app with Business Profile connected** | Ask Gemini at gemini.google.com to "help me draft a reply to my latest review" | Google's help page lists requirements rather than a price: owner or manager of only one verified profile, age 18+, a personal Google Account, Gemini Apps history on |
| **Review tools in your booking or POS software** | Some platforms include AI reply suggestions | Varies; check your provider |

According to Google's help page on managing a Business Profile with the Gemini web app, Gemini can "draft or delete replies to customer feedback". It isn't available if you have access to more than one verified profile, and you can't use it with a work or school account.

## Step 3: Use a prompt with rules

These prompts are written to keep replies short, varied and free of invented promises.

**1. Positive review**

```
Write a reply to this Google review for my business,
[business name], a [type of business] in [city].

Review: "[paste review]"
Reviewer's first name: [name, or leave out]

Rules:
- Under 50 words, friendly and natural, not salesy.
- Mention one specific thing they praised.
- No discounts, no links, no hashtags, no emojis.
- Don't invent staff names, events or details.
Give me 3 different versions.
```

**2. Negative review (you know what happened)**

```
Help me reply to this negative Google review for [business name].

Review: "[paste review]"
What actually happened: [your facts]
What I can offer: [e.g. "a call with me", "a replacement", or "nothing beyond an apology"]
Contact for follow-up: [general email or phone]

Rules:
- Under 80 words, calm and professional, never defensive.
- Thank them, acknowledge the problem, apologize only for
  what we actually got wrong, explain briefly without excuses,
  and invite them to continue the conversation offline.
- Don't share any private details about the customer or order.
- Don't promise anything beyond what I listed.
- Don't mention reviews, ratings or ask them to change the review.
```

**3. Negative review you think is unfair**

```
This review is, in my view, inaccurate: "[paste review]".
My side of the story: [facts].
Write a short, polite public reply (under 70 words) that
corrects the facts calmly without calling the reviewer a liar,
and offers to talk directly at [contact].
Then tell me separately if anything in my draft could come
across as hostile to other customers reading it.
```

If a review breaks Google's policies (for example it's spam, off-topic or from someone who was never a customer), you can report it through your Business Profile instead of arguing in public.

**4. Calm down my own draft**

```
Here's the reply I wrote while annoyed. Keep my facts and my
offer, but make it calm, short and professional. Remove anything
sarcastic, anything that blames the customer and any private details.
[paste your draft]
```

**5. Several reviews at once**

```
Below are [number] Google reviews, one per line, with star ratings.
Write a reply for each (under 50 words for 4-5 stars, under 80 for
1-3 stars), each worded differently. Use only details from the
review itself. For any 1-3 star review, add a note saying what
facts I should check before posting.
[paste reviews]
```

**6. Reply in another language**

```
This review is in [language]. Translate it for me, then write
a reply in [language] following these rules: [paste rules from
prompt 1 or 2]. Also give me an English version of the reply so
I can check what it says.
```

## A quick before-and-after

Review: *"Food was great but we waited 45 minutes for a table even though we had a booking."*

A rushed reply might be "Sorry, we were busy." A prompt with the facts ("two large groups overstayed; we now leave a 15-minute buffer between bookings") can produce something like:

> Thanks for the kind words about the food, and sorry about the wait. You booked, so you shouldn't have waited 45 minutes. Two large groups ran long that evening, and we've since added more time between bookings so it doesn't happen again. We hope to see you back soon.

Notice it contains only things the owner said were true. If you hadn't really changed the booking gap, that line would have to go.

## Step 4: Check before you post

- **Every fact is real**: dates, names, what you changed, what you're offering.
- **No private information** about the customer, their order or their health.
- **No incentive** to change or remove a review.
- **It doesn't sound copy-pasted.** Change a phrase or two; readers notice twenty identical replies.
- **You'd be happy for a future customer to read it.** That's the real audience.

## Don't let AI write reviews

Using AI to write replies is fine. Using it to write reviews of your own business, or fake negative reviews of a competitor, is not. Google's policies prohibit fake engagement, and in the United States the Federal Trade Commission's rule on consumer reviews, announced in August 2024, bans fake reviews and testimonials, including ones generated by AI, and allows civil penalties for knowing violations.

## FAQ

**Is it OK to use AI to reply to Google reviews?**
Yes. Nothing in Google's help pages forbids AI-drafted replies, and Google itself offers drafting through the Gemini web app. What matters is that the reply follows Google's content policies and is accurate. You are responsible for what you post.

**Can AI reply to my reviews automatically?**
Some third-party tools offer automatic replies, but posting without reading is risky: an AI can apologize for something that didn't happen or miss a serious complaint. Google's Gemini feature drafts replies for you to review. Reading each reply before it goes out takes seconds and avoids embarrassing mistakes.

**Should I reply to every review?**
Google's tips encourage replying, and say to make each reply count rather than posting the same "thank you" everywhere. Short, specific replies to positive reviews and timely, careful replies to negative ones are a reasonable goal.

**How long should a review reply be?**
Google's advice is to keep it short and simple. Two or three sentences usually work for positive reviews, and up to about four or five for negative ones. Long replies to complaints tend to read as defensive.

**Will customers know a reply was written with AI?**
They might if every reply is generic and full of phrases like "we truly value your feedback". Give the AI specific details, ask for several versions, and edit the one you pick so it sounds like you.

**Can I delete a bad review?**
No, but you can report a review that breaks Google's policies, and you can always reply publicly. Offering anything in exchange for removing or changing a review is prohibited.
