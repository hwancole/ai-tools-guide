Email is one of the easiest everyday jobs to hand to AI: a polite follow-up, a reply to an annoyed customer, a request to your landlord, a message to your child's teacher. The trick is to give the AI the facts and the goal, keep the draft short, and always read it before sending, because AI will happily invent a date, a promise or an apology you never meant to make.

> **Quick answer:** If you use Gmail and have a Google AI plan or a work/school Google Workspace account, click **Help me write** in the compose window and describe the email. In Outlook with Microsoft 365 Personal, Family or Premium, select the **Copilot** icon and choose **Draft**. With no paid plan at all, open ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini (free), paste your bullet points and the email you're replying to, and use one of the prompts below. Then check every name, date and number yourself.

Feature names, plans and availability below are as of October 2026 and come from Google's and Microsoft's own help pages (linked at the end).

## What to give the AI (the 30-second prep)

The difference between a generic AI email and a useful one is almost always the input. Before you ask, jot down:

1. **The goal.** What should the reader do after reading? Approve, reply by Friday, send a document, accept an apology.
2. **Who it's for.** Your manager, a client, a stranger, a professor. Mention the relationship.
3. **The facts in bullet points.** Dates, amounts, order numbers, what already happened. AI can only be accurate about facts you give it.
4. **The tone.** Friendly, formal, firm, apologetic, brief.
5. **The email you're replying to**, if it's a reply.

Leave out passwords, ID numbers, health details or confidential business information unless your organization has approved the tool for that data.

## Option 1: Help me write in Gmail

Gmail's built-in writing assistant, powered by Gemini, drafts an email from a short description or rewrites one you've already typed.

**Who has it (as of October 2026):**

| Account | Help me write | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Google AI Plus, Pro or Ultra plan | Yes | Global, in English, French, German, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese and Spanish |
| Eligible Google Workspace (work or school) plan | Yes | Global, same languages |
| Free personal Google Account | Yes | **US only**, same languages |

**How to use it on a computer:**

1. Open Gmail and click **Compose**, or open the email you want to reply to and click **Reply**.
2. Type your request in the prompt bar at the bottom of the compose window. If you don't see it, click **Help me write**.
3. Describe the email, for example: *"Ask Sam to send the signed contract by Friday, friendly but clear."*
4. Click **Create**.
5. Refine it with the quick options **Formalize**, **Friendly** or **Shorten**, or type your own instruction such as *"make it more direct."*
6. Insert the draft, edit it, and send.

You can also type a rough version of the email yourself first, then use Help me write to rework it. The feature works in the Gmail apps on Android and iPhone too.

## Option 2: Copilot in Outlook

Microsoft includes Copilot in the **Microsoft 365 Personal, Family and Premium** plans, and in Outlook it can draft new emails for you. Two limits to know: these consumer plans use **AI credits** for Copilot features, and on Family and Premium the AI features in Outlook work only for the subscription owner, not the people they share the plan with. At work, Copilot depends on what your company's license and IT admin allow.

**How to draft an email in the new Outlook:**

1. Select **Home > New mail**.
2. Select the **Copilot** icon in the toolbar and choose **Draft** (or click **Draft with Copilot** in the message area).
3. Type what the email should say, or pick a suggested prompt.
4. Select the **Generate** arrow.
5. Use the dropdown to change the **length** or **tone**, or write a new prompt and generate again.
6. Select **Keep it**, edit the draft, and send.

If Draft with Copilot doesn't appear in the new Outlook or Outlook on the web, check that your message format is HTML: go to **Settings > Mail > Compose**. Draft with Copilot doesn't support plain text.

## Option 3: Any free AI chatbot

If you don't pay for any of the above, a free chatbot works just as well for writing; you just copy the result into your email app. ChatGPT (chatgpt.com), Claude (claude.ai), Gemini (gemini.google.com) and Microsoft Copilot (copilot.microsoft.com) all offer free plans that handle this.

1. Start a new chat.
2. Paste the email you're answering, if any, and your bullet-point notes.
3. Use one of the prompts below.
4. Ask for changes in the same chat: *"shorter,"* *"less apologetic,"* *"add a subject line."*
5. Copy the final version into your email app, fix the formatting, and read it once more.

This option is also the best for longer or sensitive emails, because you can explain the background in detail and ask the AI to point out risks before it writes.

## Copy-paste prompts

**1. Turn notes into an email**

> Write an email to [person / role] from my notes below. Goal: [what I want them to do]. Tone: [friendly / formal / firm]. Keep it under 150 words, with a clear subject line and the request in the first two sentences. Use only the facts in my notes; if something important is missing, ask me instead of guessing.
>
> Notes: [your bullet points]

**2. Reply to an email**

> Here is an email I received: [paste it]. Write a reply that [agrees / politely declines / asks for more time / answers their questions]. Answer every question they asked, in the same order. Keep my reply shorter than their email.

**3. Polite follow-up when someone hasn't answered**

> Write a short, polite follow-up to [person] about [topic]. I first wrote on [date] asking for [what]. Don't sound annoyed or guilt them. Make it easy to answer with a yes/no or a date.

**4. Say no without burning the bridge**

> Help me decline [request] from [person]. My real reason: [reason]. I want to stay on good terms. Offer [an alternative / a later date] if possible. No over-apologizing, under 120 words.

**5. Handle a complaint or a tense situation**

> I need to reply to this upset email from a [customer / colleague / neighbor]: [paste it]. The facts are: [what actually happened]. Write a calm, professional reply that acknowledges their concern, explains what happened without blaming anyone, and says what happens next. Don't promise refunds, deadlines or anything not in my facts.

**6. Fix an email I already wrote**

> Here is my draft: [paste it]. Make it clearer and more professional, but keep my voice and all the facts. Point out anything that could be misread or sound rude before you rewrite it.

**7. Write in another language**

> Translate and adapt this email for a reader in [country], in [language]. Use the level of formality that's normal for a [business / school / official] email there. Keep names, dates and numbers exactly the same.

## Checklist before you hit send

- **Names and spelling.** The recipient's name, company and job title.
- **Dates, times and time zones.** AI sometimes turns "next Friday" into a specific date that's wrong.
- **Numbers.** Amounts, order numbers, phone numbers. Compare them with your notes.
- **Promises.** Delete any commitment, discount or apology the AI added that you didn't ask for.
- **Length.** Most work emails do best when the main point fits on a phone screen. Cut the warm-up lines.
- **Tone.** Read it out loud. If you'd never say a sentence in person, rewrite it.
- **Recipients and attachments.** AI doesn't check the To, Cc and attachment fields. You do.

## Common mistakes

- **Asking with no facts.** "Write an email to my boss about the project" gets you vague filler. Give the bullet points.
- **Letting AI make it longer.** Ask for a word limit; short emails get answered faster.
- **Sending replies that ignore the question.** Paste the original email so the AI answers what was actually asked.
- **Over-formal language.** Phrases like "I hope this email finds you well" and "please do not hesitate to reach out" make an email sound templated. Ask the AI to drop them.
- **Pasting confidential work data into a personal account.** If it's company information, use the tool your employer has approved.

## FAQ

**Is Help me write in Gmail free?**
For personal Google Accounts it's available at no cost only in the US, as of October 2026. Elsewhere you need a Google AI Plus, Pro or Ultra plan or an eligible Google Workspace account.

**Why don't I see Help me write or Copilot in my email?**
In Gmail, check your account type and country against the table above. In Outlook, Copilot needs Microsoft 365 Personal, Family or Premium (on Family and Premium, only the subscription owner gets it) or a work license your admin has enabled. In the new Outlook, also make sure the message format is HTML, not plain text.

**Can people tell an email was written by AI?**
Often, when it's long, generic and full of stock phrases. A short email with your specific facts, edited in your own words, reads like any other email.

**Is it okay to use AI for work emails?**
Usually yes, for drafting and editing, but follow your employer's rules on which tools can see company information. You're still responsible for what you send.

**Which is best for emails: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot?**
For everyday emails they're all capable. The built-in option in your email app saves copying and pasting; a chatbot is better when you need to explain a lot of background. Pick based on convenience, not brand.

**Can AI write an email in a language I don't speak well?**
Yes, and it's a good use of it. Say which country the reader is in so the formality fits, and keep the email simple. For anything important, have a fluent speaker check it.
