ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Copilot all have a paid plan at roughly $20 a month, and from a distance they look the same. They aren't. Each one bundles different extras, and the best choice depends less on which AI is "smartest" and more on what you'll actually use it for.

> **Quick answer (US prices as of November 2026):**
> - **ChatGPT Plus ($20/month):** the best all-rounder if you want image creation, deep research, custom GPTs and scheduled tasks in one app.
> - **Claude Pro ($20/month, or $17/month billed yearly):** the best pick for long documents, careful writing and coding (it includes Claude Code).
> - **Google AI Pro ($19.99/month):** the best value if you live in Gmail, Docs and Drive. It also includes 5 TB of storage, video generation and YouTube Premium Lite.
> - **Microsoft 365 Premium ($19.99/month):** the pick if you already pay for Office. It adds Copilot inside Word, Excel, PowerPoint and Outlook.
>
> If you only chat a few times a week, **stay on the free plans first**. All four free versions are good enough for everyday questions.

All four vendors cap usage even on paid plans ("limits apply"), and none of them publishes an exact message count. Expect generous limits for normal daily use, and slowdowns only if you run long, heavy sessions all day.

## ChatGPT Plus: the most features in one place

OpenAI's pricing page describes Plus as "Do more with advanced intelligence". Compared with the free plan, it adds:

- **Advanced reasoning models** (the free plan has unlimited standard chats, but reasoning is limited)
- **Expanded messages, uploads and deep research**
- **Projects, scheduled tasks and custom GPTs**
- **Expanded Codex** for coding
- **Early access** to new features

**Choose it if:** you want one app that does a bit of everything: answer questions, make images, research a topic, analyze a spreadsheet and remind you of things on a schedule. It's also the easiest to recommend to someone who has never paid for AI before, because most tutorials online assume ChatGPT.

**Skip it if:** you mostly work in Google or Microsoft apps and want the AI inside them, not in a separate window.

**Cheaper and pricier options:** OpenAI also sells **Go**, a lower-cost plan with more messages, uploads and image creation than Free (the pricing page says it "may include ads"), and **Pro**, which comes in three usage tiers with monthly API credits. Prices for those vary by country, so check [chatgpt.com/pricing](https://chatgpt.com/pricing/) for yours.

## Claude Pro: the strongest pick for writing and long documents

Anthropic's pricing page lists Claude Pro at **$20 a month**, or **$17 a month** if you pay **$200 for a year** up front. Compared with Free, it adds:

- **More usage** (the free plan resets every 5 hours and has a weekly cap)
- **Opus** and more Claude models (the free plan uses Sonnet and Haiku)
- **Research**, Anthropic's web research mode
- **Claude Code**, for programming
- **Claude in Chrome and Microsoft 365**
- **Scheduled tasks** and handing off work

Every Claude plan, free included, lists a context window of **up to 1 million tokens**, which means Claude can work with very long files in one conversation.

**Choose it if:** you write a lot (reports, essays, emails, fiction), paste in long contracts or research papers, or code. Claude is also a good second opinion if you already use another chatbot.

**Skip it if:** you need image or video generation. Claude can read images but doesn't create pictures the way ChatGPT and Gemini do.

**If Pro runs out:** Anthropic also sells **Max at $100 a month** (5x Pro usage) and **Ultra at $200 a month** (10x Pro usage, no 5-hour limit for verified accounts). Most people don't need these.

## Google AI Pro: the best value if you use Google apps

Google AI Pro costs **$19.99 a month** or **$199.99 a year**. Google says it gives you **4x the usage of the free plan**, plus:

- Google's top Gemini models and **Deep Research**
- **Video generation** and the **Daily Brief**
- **1,000 Google Flow credits** for AI video
- Gemini built into **Gmail, Docs, Drive** and other Google apps
- **5 TB** of cloud storage across Gmail, Drive and Photos
- **YouTube Premium Lite** and **Google Home Premium Standard**
- A **1 million token context window** and uploads up to 1,500 pages

**Choose it if:** you already pay Google for storage, or you'd use the bundle. Once you count the storage and YouTube Premium Lite, this is the most stuff for the money. It's also the natural pick if your email, documents and photos are all in Google.

**Skip it if:** you don't use Google apps and don't need the storage. Then you're paying for extras you'll ignore.

**Cheaper and pricier options:** **Google AI Plus** ($4.99 a month as of November 2026) gives 2x free usage, video generation, 400 GB of storage and Gemini in Gmail. **Google AI Ultra** starts at **$99.99 a month**. Plus is one of the cheapest paid AI plans available if you just want a bit more than free.

## Microsoft 365 Premium: for people who live in Office

Microsoft no longer sells Copilot Pro on its own for home users. Instead, Copilot comes with Microsoft 365 plans. **Microsoft 365 Premium** costs **$19.99 a month** or **$199.99 a year** and includes "extensive" use of Copilot chat, image creation and voice, plus agentic features for research and analysis, on top of the Office apps.

Cheaper Microsoft 365 plans also include some Copilot: **Personal** ($9.99/month) and **Family** ($12.99/month, Copilot for the account holder only) get "higher usage than free".

**Choose it if:** you'd pay for Word, Excel and PowerPoint anyway and want an assistant that can draft a document, build a formula or turn notes into slides without leaving the app.

**Skip it if:** you don't use Office. The AI alone isn't a reason to pick this bundle.

## How to decide without wasting money

1. **Use the free versions for a week.** Do the tasks you'd actually pay for: your real emails, your real spreadsheets, your real homework.
2. **Notice what stops you.** Hitting a message limit? Need longer files? Want images? That tells you which upgrade matters.
3. **Pay for one month, not a year.** Monthly billing lets you cancel. Switch to yearly only once you know you'll keep it.
4. **Run the same test in two tools.** Paste this into each free version and compare:

```
I'm deciding which AI assistant to pay for. Here is a real task I do every week:
[describe the task and paste a sample, with personal details removed]
Do the task. Then list any part you couldn't do well, and say what
information or file would help you do it better.
```

5. **Check what you already pay for.** If you have Google One storage or Microsoft 365, upgrading that plan may cost less than adding a new subscription.

Before you paste work or personal files into any of them, see [how to stop AI training on your chats](https://hwancole.github.io/ai-tools-guide/en/stop-ai-training-on-your-chats/).

## FAQ

**Is ChatGPT Plus worth $20 a month?**
It is if you use ChatGPT most days and keep running into limits, or you want image creation, deep research and custom GPTs without caps that feel tight. If you use it a few times a week, the free plan is probably enough.

**Is Claude better than ChatGPT?**
Neither is better at everything. Claude stands out for long documents (every plan lists a context window of up to 1M tokens) and careful writing; ChatGPT has more built-in tools, such as image creation. Try both free versions on your own tasks.

**Is Gemini free?**
Yes. Gemini's free plan includes image generation, Deep Research, Gemini Live and 15 GB of storage (as of November 2026). Google AI Plus and Pro add higher usage, video generation and more storage.

**Can I share one AI subscription with my family?**
The individual plans are for one person. Microsoft 365 Family covers up to six people for Office, but its Copilot features are for the account holder only. For Google and the others, check the vendor's plan page for family sharing rules before you buy.

**Are there student discounts?**
Vendors run student offers from time to time, and they change often. Check each vendor's pricing page before you pay full price.

**Do prices differ outside the US?**
Yes. All four companies set local prices, and taxes are added in many countries. The figures here are US prices as of November 2026.
