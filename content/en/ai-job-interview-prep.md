Most interview prep advice says "practice out loud." The hard part is finding someone to practice with. An AI chatbot can play the interviewer: it reads the job posting, asks you realistic questions, listens to your answers (by voice or text) and tells you what was vague, too long or missing. It can't tell you what *this* company's interviewers will really ask, and it doesn't know your past better than you do, but as a practice partner it's available at 11 p.m. the night before and never gets bored.

> **Quick answer:** Open ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini (free plans are enough). Paste the job posting and your resume, and ask for the 10 questions you're most likely to get. Prepare 4 to 6 real stories from your experience in STAR format (situation, task, action, result). Then switch to voice mode on your phone and ask the AI to interview you one question at a time and give feedback after each answer. Finish by preparing 3 questions to ask the interviewer.

## What AI is good at (and what it isn't)

| AI helps with | Do this yourself |
|---|---|
| Predicting common and role-specific questions from the job posting | Choosing which real stories to tell |
| Turning a rambling story into a clear STAR answer | Checking that every detail in your answer is true |
| Playing the interviewer in a spoken mock interview | Confirming facts about the company on its own site and recent news |
| Spotting filler, vague claims and answers that run long | Deciding on salary expectations (use real salary data, not a chatbot's guess) |
| Suggesting smart questions to ask at the end | Practicing with a real person at least once if you can |

A chatbot can sound confident about a company's history, products or interview process and still be wrong. Use it to *organize* your research, not to replace it.

## Step by step

### 1. Give the AI the job and your background

Start a new chat in ChatGPT (chatgpt.com), Claude (claude.ai) or Gemini (gemini.google.com). Paste the full job posting, then paste or upload your resume. If you have notes about the company (from its careers page, About page or a recent announcement), paste those too, so the AI works from real material.

### 2. Predict the questions

Ask for the questions you're most likely to get, grouped by type: general ("tell me about yourself"), behavioral ("tell me about a time..."), role-specific or technical, and questions about gaps or career changes. Use prompt 1 below.

### 3. Build your story bank

Most behavioral questions can be answered with the same 4 to 6 stories, told from different angles: a problem you solved, a conflict you handled, a mistake you learned from, a time you led or took initiative, and a result you're proud of. Write rough notes for each, then ask the AI to shape them into STAR answers of about 60 to 90 seconds when spoken (prompt 2).

### 4. Run a mock interview by voice

Reading answers on a screen is not the same as saying them. All three major apps have a voice mode, so you can speak your answers and hear the next question (details in the table below). Use prompt 3 and ask the AI to wait for your full answer before giving feedback. If voice isn't practical, the same prompt works by typing.

### 5. Review and tighten

After the session, ask for a summary of your weakest answers and do those again (prompt 4). Two or three short sessions over a few days usually work better than one long one the night before.

### 6. Prepare your questions and logistics

Ask for good questions to ask the interviewer (prompt 6), then pick the 3 you actually care about. Confirm the time zone, video link or address, and who you're meeting yourself, from the invitation email.

## Copy-paste prompts

**1. Predict the questions**

> Here is a job posting and my resume. Act as the hiring manager for this role. List the 12 questions I'm most likely to be asked, grouped into: general, behavioral, role-specific, and questions about weak spots in my resume. For each one, add one sentence on what the interviewer is really trying to find out.

**2. Turn my notes into a STAR answer**

> Here are rough notes about something I did: [your notes]. Turn them into an answer to "[interview question]" using the STAR format (situation, task, action, result). Keep it to about 150 words so it takes around a minute to say. Use only the facts in my notes; if a key detail is missing, such as the result, ask me instead of inventing it.

**3. Mock interview (use with voice mode)**

> Let's do a mock interview for the [job title] role at [company], based on the posting and resume above. You are the interviewer. Ask me one question at a time and wait for my full answer. After each answer, give me short feedback: one thing that worked, one thing to improve, and whether the answer was too long. Then ask the next question. Mix general, behavioral and role-specific questions, and ask a follow-up question when my answer is vague, like a real interviewer would. Start when I say "ready."

**4. Find my weak spots**

> Based on the mock interview so far, which 3 of my answers were weakest and why? For each one, show me a stronger structure (not a script), and point out any filler words, vague claims or missing results.

**5. Tough or awkward questions**

> Help me prepare honest, short answers to these questions: [for example "Why did you leave your last job?", "What's your biggest weakness?", "Why is there a gap in your resume?"]. My real situation is: [explain briefly]. Keep each answer under 60 seconds, positive but truthful, and don't make up reasons.

**6. Questions to ask the interviewer**

> Suggest 8 thoughtful questions I could ask at the end of an interview for this role. Avoid anything I could easily find on the company's website. Include questions about the team, how success is measured in the first 90 days, and the next steps in the process.

**7. Practice in another language**

> Interview me in [English / Spanish / another language] for this role, at a professional level. After each answer, correct any grammar or word choice that would sound unnatural to a native-speaking interviewer, then continue.

## Which app to use for spoken practice

The method works the same in every chatbot. These are the voice features as of October 2026, from each company's help pages:

| | ChatGPT | Claude | Gemini |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Voice feature** | Voice mode | Voice mode (labeled beta) | Gemini Live |
| **Free plan** | Yes, with daily usage limits; paid plans get more | Yes, on all plans; voice counts toward your normal usage limits | Yes, in the free app; paid plans get more access |
| **Where** | iOS and Android apps, and chatgpt.com in the browser | iOS, Android, desktop app and claude.ai on the web | Gemini app on Android and iPhone; not in the Gemini web app |
| **How to start** | Tap or click the voice icon next to the message box | Tap or click the sound-wave icon next to the message box | Tap **Live** in the Gemini app |

Two practical tips: start voice mode *in the same chat* where you pasted the job posting and resume, so the AI keeps that context; and use a quiet room or headphones so it doesn't cut you off mid-answer. Claude's voice mode also has a push-to-talk option for noisy places. When a Gemini Live session ends, you get a transcript you can review.

## Getting useful feedback (not just praise)

Chatbots tend to be encouraging. To get feedback you can use:

- **Ask for specific criteria**: clarity, structure, evidence, length and relevance to the job.
- **Ask it to be strict**: "Score each answer from 1 to 5 as a demanding hiring manager would, and explain any score below 4."
- **Ask what's missing**, not just what's wrong: "What would a strong candidate have said that I didn't?"
- **Repeat the weak answers** right away, then compare the two versions.

Record yourself on your phone once, too. Listening back shows habits that text feedback misses, like speaking too fast or trailing off at the end of answers.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Memorizing an AI script word for word.** It sounds rehearsed, and you'll freeze if the question is phrased differently. Memorize the *points* of each story, not the sentences.
- **Telling stories that aren't yours.** If the AI "improves" a result or adds a responsibility you didn't have, remove it. Interviewers ask follow-up questions.
- **Trusting AI facts about the company.** Check names, products and recent news on the company's own website and reliable news sources.
- **Using AI during a live interview.** Many employers treat this as cheating, and reading from a screen is usually obvious. Prepare beforehand instead.
- **Sharing confidential details.** Don't paste internal documents from a current employer or other people's personal information into a chatbot.

## FAQ

**Can AI really do a mock interview?**
Yes. With voice mode, ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini can ask questions out loud, listen to your spoken answer and respond. They're good practice partners for common and behavioral questions. They can't fully simulate a specific company's interviewers, so treat the questions as likely examples, not a leak of the real ones.

**Is it free?**
As of October 2026, voice conversations are available on the free plans of ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini, with usage limits that vary by service. For a 20 to 30 minute practice session, the free tier is usually enough; if you hit a limit, continue by typing.

**Which is best for interview practice: ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini?**
Any of them works well with the prompts above. Use the app you already have on your phone. If you want a second opinion on your answers, paste the same answer into another chatbot and compare the feedback.

**How long should my interview answers be?**
For most behavioral questions, about one to two minutes when spoken. Ask the AI to flag any answer that runs much longer, and practice cutting the setup (situation) short so you spend more time on what you did and the result.

**Can AI help with technical or case interviews?**
It can generate practice questions and explain concepts, and for coding interviews it can review your solution. Check its explanations against reliable sources, because chatbots can make mistakes on technical details.

**Can I use AI to practice for an interview in English if it's not my first language?**
Yes, and voice mode is especially useful for this. Use prompt 7 so it corrects your phrasing after each answer, and ask it to speak a little slower if you need to.
