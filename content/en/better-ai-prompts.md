Most disappointing AI answers come from short, vague prompts. The AI isn't reading your mind; it's filling in every gap with the most generic guess. The fix is simple and works the same way in ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot or any other chatbot: give it the details a smart new colleague would need.

> **Quick answer:** A good prompt has four parts: **Role** (who the AI should act as), **Context** (your situation and audience), **Task** (exactly what to do) and **Format** (what the answer should look like). Then treat the first answer as a draft and ask for changes.

## The 4-part prompt formula

| Part | What to include | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Role** | The expertise you want | "Act as a patient math tutor." |
| **Context** | Who you are, who it's for, what you already tried | "My daughter is 12 and struggles with fractions." |
| **Task** | One clear action, with any must-haves | "Explain how to add 1/3 and 1/4 step by step." |
| **Format** | Length, structure, tone | "Use a short story with pizza slices, under 150 words." |

You don't need fancy wording or "magic phrases". Plain, specific language beats clever tricks.

## Before and after

**Vague prompt:**

> Write an email to my landlord about the heating.

**Better prompt:**

> You're helping a tenant write a polite but firm email. My heating has been broken for 5 days, I reported it by phone on Monday and nobody came. I want a repair date this week. Write a short email (under 150 words) that states the facts, mentions my earlier call, and asks for a specific date. Friendly tone, no threats.

The second version gives the AI the facts, the goal and the tone, so the first draft is usually close to usable.

## 7 habits that make every prompt better

1. **Paste the material.** If you want a summary, rewrite or answer about a document, paste the text (or upload the file if your tool supports it) instead of describing it.
2. **Say who it's for.** "For a 10-year-old", "for my boss", or "for a job application in marketing" changes vocabulary and tone more than anything else.
3. **Give an example.** Show one sample of the style you like: "Write it like this: …". Examples work better than adjectives.
4. **Ask for a specific format.** Bullet list, table, numbered steps, 3 options, under 100 words. Formats make answers easier to use.
5. **Let it ask you questions.** Add: "Before you start, ask me up to 3 questions if anything is unclear." This alone fixes many bad first answers.
6. **Iterate instead of restarting.** Reply with "shorter", "more casual", "make point 2 more specific" or "give me 3 different versions". The AI remembers the conversation.
7. **Break big jobs into steps.** For a long report, first ask for an outline, approve it, then ask for each section.

## Copy-paste templates

**Summarize something long**

> Summarize the text below for [audience]. Give me: 1) a 2-sentence overview, 2) the 5 most important points as bullets, 3) any deadlines or numbers I must not miss. Text: [paste]

**Improve your writing**

> Edit my text for clarity and grammar. Keep my voice and meaning, don't add new information. Then list the 3 biggest changes you made and why. Text: [paste]

**Learn something new**

> Explain [topic] to me as if I'm a smart beginner. Start with a one-paragraph overview, then the 3 key ideas with everyday examples, then a 5-question quiz to check my understanding. Wait for my answers before giving the solutions.

**Plan a trip or project**

> Help me plan [trip/project]. Constraints: [budget, dates, people, must-dos]. Give me a day-by-day (or week-by-week) plan in a table, and flag anything I should book or decide early.

**Make a decision**

> I'm choosing between [A] and [B]. My priorities are [list]. Compare them in a table against my priorities, then tell me which you'd pick and what would change your mind.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Trusting facts blindly.** AI chatbots can state wrong facts, quotes, prices or sources with total confidence. Check anything important, especially numbers, legal, medical or financial details, against a reliable source.
- **Sharing private data.** Don't paste passwords, ID numbers, or confidential work documents unless your organization has approved that tool for it. Check your tool's privacy and data settings.
- **Cramming many tasks into one prompt.** "Summarize this, translate it, write a tweet and make a quiz" gives you four mediocre results. Do them one by one.
- **Giving up after one answer.** The first answer is a draft. Two or three short follow-ups usually get you something much better.

## Does this work in every AI tool?

Yes. ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Microsoft Copilot, Meta AI and other chatbots all respond better to clear role, context, task and format. Some tools also let you save standing instructions (often called custom instructions, a project, or a gem) so you don't have to repeat your context each time. If you find yourself typing the same background again and again, put it there.

## FAQ

**Do I need to be polite to the AI?** It doesn't need politeness, but clear, complete sentences tend to produce clearer answers. Write the way you'd brief a capable assistant.

**How long should a prompt be?** As long as it needs to be. One sentence is fine for a quick fact; a few short paragraphs are normal for writing or analysis tasks.

**What if the answer is still bad?** Tell it exactly what's wrong ("too formal", "you missed the deadline in paragraph 2") and ask it to try again. If it keeps missing, start a new chat with a clearer prompt that includes what you learned.
