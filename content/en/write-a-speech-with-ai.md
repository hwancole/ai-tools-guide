AI is a good speechwriting partner for the parts most people find hardest: getting started, putting stories in order, cutting the speech to length and finding a closing line. What it can't do is supply the material that makes a speech land, because it doesn't know the people in the room. A speech that sounds generic almost always started with a generic prompt. The fix is to let the AI interview you first, then write from your answers.

> **Quick answer (as of November 2026):** Open ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini (the free plans are enough) and paste: *"I need to give a [3-minute] [wedding toast / retirement speech / eulogy] for [who]. Before writing anything, ask me 8 questions, one at a time, to collect specific stories and details. Then write a draft in my voice, about [400] words, that I can read aloud."* Answer with real details, ask for a shorter or warmer version, then rehearse it out loud, ideally with the app's voice mode, and cut anything that doesn't sound like you.

## Why the interview step matters

If you type "write a best man speech for my brother Tom", you get a speech that could be about anyone: a joke about how he "finally found someone who puts up with him", a line about love being patient, a toast. Guests can hear that kind of filler.

The material that works is specific: the time he drove four hours to help you move, the nickname only the family uses, the moment you knew the couple was serious. You have those details; the AI has the structure. Asking it to interview you before writing gets the two together.

## Step 1: Gather the basics

Before you open a chatbot, have these ready. It takes five minutes and saves a lot of rewriting.

| Detail | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Occasion and your role** | A best man, a parent of the bride and a coworker all speak differently |
| **Time limit** | Sets the word count (see the table below) |
| **Audience** | Family only, mixed ages, colleagues, a client: changes which jokes are safe |
| **Tone** | Funny, heartfelt, formal, or a mix |
| **Two or three real stories** | The heart of the speech |
| **Names to thank or mention** | Easy to forget under pressure |
| **Anything off-limits** | Exes, money, health, in-jokes that would embarrass someone |

### How long should the speech be?

People speak at different speeds, so treat these as rough starting points and time yourself reading the draft aloud.

| Speaking time | Approximate words |
|---|---|
| 1 minute | 120 to 150 |
| 2 minutes | 250 to 300 |
| 3 minutes | 375 to 450 |
| 5 minutes | 625 to 750 |

Most toasts at weddings and parties work best at two to four minutes. Work presentations and eulogies can run longer, but shorter is rarely a problem.

## Step 2: Let the AI interview you

Paste this into any chatbot and fill in the brackets:

```
I need to give a speech and I want your help writing it.

Occasion: [wedding / 50th birthday / retirement / funeral / team event]
My role: [best man / maid of honor / father of the bride / manager / friend]
About: [name and relationship to me]
Length: [3] minutes spoken
Audience: [about 100 guests, mixed ages, mostly family]
Tone: [warm with a few light jokes]
Avoid: [mentions of exes, drinking stories, anything about work]

Before you write anything, interview me. Ask me one question
at a time, up to 8 questions, to collect specific stories,
details and feelings. Wait for my answer before the next
question. When you have enough, tell me and write the draft.
```

Answer in full sentences and include small details: where you were, what someone said, how you felt. You can type or, on a phone, dictate your answers, which often produces more natural stories than typing.

## Step 3: Get a draft with a clear structure

When the interview is done, the AI will usually write a draft on its own. If it doesn't, or the structure is weak, ask for this shape:

```
Write the speech using this structure:
1. Opening: who I am and how I know them (2 sentences max)
2. One main story that shows who [name] is
3. A second, shorter story or detail (for a wedding: about
   the couple together)
4. What I wish for them / what they mean to us
5. A closing line that leads into a toast (or a thank-you)

Write it the way I talk: short sentences, plain words,
no clichés like "for those who don't know me" or quotes from
famous people. Mark where to pause with [pause].
About [400] words.
```

The "write it the way I talk" line matters. Without it, chatbots tend toward polished, wordy prose that sounds fine on screen and stiff out loud.

## Step 4: Edit it until it sounds like you

Read the draft aloud once before you change anything. Then use follow-ups like these:

- *"Cut this to 300 words without losing the story about the camping trip."*
- *"The joke in paragraph 2 could offend his grandmother. Give me three gentler options."*
- *"Make the ending more emotional but keep it under three sentences."*
- *"Replace any phrase that sounds like a greeting card with something plainer."*
- *"Give me five different closing lines to choose from."*
- *"Check: is there anything here that could embarrass someone or reveal something private?"*

Change any word you wouldn't naturally say. If you find yourself stumbling over a sentence when reading aloud, split it in two or rewrite it yourself. Facts, names and dates are your job to check; the AI will fill gaps with plausible guesses if your answers left something out.

## Prompts for specific speeches

**Wedding toast or best man / maid of honor speech**

```
Help me write a [3]-minute [best man] speech. Include one funny
story about [groom] that's safe for grandparents, one moment
that shows [groom] and [partner] are right for each other, a
welcome to [partner]'s family, and end with a toast to the
couple. Keep the teasing affectionate, never mean.
```

**Eulogy**

```
I'm giving a eulogy for my [grandmother], [name], about
[5] minutes. Interview me gently, one question at a time,
about her life, what she cared about and small habits people
will recognize. Then write a draft that's honest and warm, can
include one lighter memory, and ends with what she taught us.
Avoid religious language unless I mention it.
```

**Retirement or farewell speech (as the speaker or the honoree)**

```
Write a [2]-minute speech for [name]'s retirement after
[25] years at [company/team]. I'm [their manager]. Mention
[two specific achievements or traits], one light story from
the office, and thank them on behalf of the team. Professional
but personal, no corporate buzzwords.
```

**Short work talk or presentation opening**

```
I'm presenting [topic] to [audience] for [10] minutes. Write a
60-second opening that states the problem, why it matters to
this audience and what they'll get from the next 10 minutes.
Then outline the rest as 4 to 5 talking points, not a script.
```

For longer work talks, an outline you speak from usually comes across better than a word-for-word script.

## Step 5: Rehearse with AI

Writing is only half of it. A few ways AI helps with practice:

- **Voice mode as a listener.** Claude's voice mode is available on all plans, including Free, in the mobile apps, desktop app and web, and voice chats count toward your normal usage limits (as of November 2026). In the Gemini mobile app on Android you tap **Live** at the bottom to talk; ChatGPT's mobile app also has a voice mode. Read the speech aloud and ask for feedback on pace and which lines sound unnatural.
- **Timing.** Record yourself on your phone, or read it while a timer runs. If you're over, ask the AI to cut a specific number of words.
- **Cue cards.** Ask: *"Turn this speech into 5 cue cards with only the key phrases and the first line of each section, so I don't read word for word."*
- **Tough spots.** Ask: *"Which sentence in this speech will be hardest to say if I get emotional? Give me a simpler version as a backup."*

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Publishing the first draft.** It will be competent and forgettable. The second and third passes, with your edits, are where it improves.
- **Letting AI invent memories.** If the draft includes a story you didn't tell it, delete it. Guests will know.
- **Inside jokes nobody gets.** Ask the AI to flag any line that needs background the audience doesn't have.
- **Borrowed quotes.** Chatbots love famous quotes and sometimes get the wording or the author wrong. If you use one, check it, or skip it.
- **Reading off a phone in a noisy room.** Print the speech or use cue cards in a large font.

## Privacy note

You'll be typing personal stories about other people. If that bothers you, leave out surnames and sensitive details in the chat and add them back in your final copy. ## FAQ

**Is it OK to use AI to write a wedding speech?**
Yes, as long as the stories and feelings are yours. Think of AI as a helper for structure and wording. Guests respond to the specific memories, which only you can provide.

**Which AI is best for writing a speech?**
ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini can all write a solid draft on their free plans. The quality depends far more on the details you give it than on the tool. Pick the one you already use.

**Will people be able to tell my speech was written by AI?**
They might if you use a generic draft: stock phrases, a famous quote and vague praise are giveaways. A speech built from your own stories and edited into your own words reads like you wrote it, because in every way that matters you did.

**How many words is a 3-minute speech?**
Roughly 375 to 450 words at a comfortable pace. Time yourself reading it aloud, since nerves tend to make people speak faster.

**Can AI help me if I'm nervous about public speaking?**
It can help you prepare: shorter sentences that are easier to say, cue cards, timed practice and backup lines for emotional moments. Rehearsing aloud several times does more for nerves than any rewrite.

**Can I ask AI to make my speech funnier?**
Yes, but give it the real situation the joke is about and ask for several options. Test each joke on someone who will be in the audience, and drop anything that depends on making someone look bad.
